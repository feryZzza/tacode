\begin{abstract}
Task-agnostic exoskeleton controllers map wearable-sensor histories directly to biological joint moments, allowing sensor faults to propagate into assistance even when nominal prediction error is low.
We formulate deployment-time reliability as a validation-calibrated risk--utility decision problem under participant, task, and sensor-fault shifts: fault evidence should change the command without unnecessarily suppressing valid assistance.

We introduce a causal multi-signal monitor that combines probabilistic moment estimation, learned predictive-consistency signals, and mechanism-specific integrity checks, then maps calibrated timestep risk to a continuous torque gate, using validation data only for normalization, detector selection, and gate tuning.
Participant-disjoint evaluation covers in-distribution and held-out tasks, the reported training-time faults, and three fault generators excluded from training and detector selection.
Across three seeds and on the unseen fault generators, the frozen detector restricted to the channels the gate reads at command time attains macro-AUROC $0.812$ in-distribution and $0.715$ on held-out tasks; allowing the two integrity channels the gate does not consume raises these to $0.842$ and $0.778$.
Relative to a split-matched deterministic TCN, reliability training increases clean-trial RMSE by $0.011$ Nm/kg in-distribution and $0.021$ Nm/kg on held-out tasks.
At the validation-selected operating point, gating reduces the proportion of active timesteps receiving wrong-direction torque by about $0.7$ percentage points while retaining $96.1\%$ of the ungated estimator's aligned torque on clean trials.
Across fifteen leave-one-subject-out folds the per-subject reduction is $0.44$ percentage points ($95\%$ bootstrap CI $[0.23, 0.67]$) and positive for every participant.
These results show that deployment reliability should be evaluated jointly through fault discrimination, control consequences, and retained nominal utility.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Powered lower-limb exoskeletons can reduce locomotor effort and support people with gait impairments, but controllers must decide what assistance is appropriate as the user moves among activities \citep{young2017state,kim2019exosuit,awad2017soft,siviy2023opportunities}.
Mode-based controllers decide from a fixed task set \citep{camargo2021classification,kang2022classification}, which scales poorly to transitions and unstructured motion; task-agnostic control instead estimates biological hip and knee moments from wearable sensors and uses this continuous physiological state to assist cyclic, transient, and unstructured movements \citep{molinaro2022hip,molinaro2024unifies,molinaro2024task}.

Wider task coverage, however, does not ensure that every deployed prediction is reliable.
Frozen streams, drift, burst packet loss, or asynchronous modalities can corrupt an otherwise familiar trial, and because predicted moments feed the torque pipeline, such errors may remove useful assistance or reverse its direction.
Clean RMSE cannot reveal these isolated events, predictive uncertainty alone need not detect a held but locally plausible value or slow drift \citep{ovadia2019trust}, and detecting an anomaly still does not specify what the actuator should do.
The question is therefore whether such an estimator can detect several forms of sensor failure and respond without unnecessarily suppressing valid assistance.

We address it with a causal multi-head TCN trained on synthetic faults, predicting joint moments, data-dependent uncertainty, fault probability, clean-input reconstruction, and short-horizon sensor values. A separate monitor combines these outputs with causal integrity checks, selects its inputs without test data, and maps timestep risk to a continuous torque gate. Participant-disjoint splits separate task shift from unseen fault generators, and we report detection together with wrong-direction torque and retained assistance.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Task-agnostic exoskeleton control.}
Conventional controllers infer a discrete locomotion mode or gait phase before applying a task-specific torque policy \citep{camargo2021classification,kang2022classification}.
Moment-based control instead uses an instantaneous biological joint-moment estimate as a continuous control state, first for subject-independent hip assistance \citep{shepherd2022deep,molinaro2022hip}, then as a common representation across ambulatory conditions \citep{molinaro2024unifies}, and finally for coordinated hip and knee assistance without task classification \citep{molinaro2024task}, whose sensing configuration, prediction target, and control transformation we adopt.
Those studies evaluate accuracy under nominal operation; their sensor ablations measure sensitivity to removing a modality but do not estimate sensor health, calibrate deployment risk, or change assistance when a fault is detected.

\paragraph{Data efficiency and domain shift.}
Moment labels require motion-capture and force-plate measurement and are device-specific, so \citet{scherpereel2025domain} translate open-source biomechanics data into a target sensor domain by CycleGAN, reducing the labeled target data needed.
Personalization \citep{zhang2017humanloop,slade2022personalizing} and simulation-trained policies \citep{luo2024simulation} likewise act before deployment.
All change how the estimator or its assistance profile is obtained; we address corrupted observations encountered afterwards on the target device.

\paragraph{Uncertainty, anomalies, and selective prediction.}
Heteroscedastic regression separates input-dependent observation noise from the predicted mean \citep{kendall2017uncertainties}, while MC dropout and deep ensembles approximate epistemic uncertainty \citep{gal2016dropout,lakshminarayanan2017ensembles}, though both may become miscalibrated under shift \citep{ovadia2019trust}.
Uncertainty-aware ankle assistance switches on OOD estimates, targeting behavioral novelty rather than structured sensor faults \citep{tourk2025uncertainty}.
For multivariate time series, reconstruction error and changes in inter-variable relationships give evidence that predictive variance misses \citep{song2023memto,dai2024sarad}, motivating mechanism-specific channels.
Selective prediction abstains when expected error is high \citep{geifman2019selectivenet}; an assistive controller cannot abstain without saying what the actuator should do, so we convert calibrated risk into a continuous gain under control-oriented constraints.

\section{Problem Formulation}

Let $\mathbf{x}_{1:T}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times T}$ denote a causal input window of unilateral measurements from the foot, shank, and thigh IMUs, the insole, and the hip and knee encoders, and let the target $\mathbf{y}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$ hold mass-normalized biological hip and knee moments.
During deployment, an unknown corruption $f\in\mathcal{F}$ may transform the observations and produce $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1:T}$.
We seek a predictor $\widehat{\mathbf{y}}_t$ and a reliability gate $g_t\in[0,1]$ such that the commanded torque
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\tau}^{\mathrm{cmd}}_t
    = g_t\,\mathcal{C}(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}_{1:t})
\end{equation}
preserves useful assistance for clean inputs while suppressing harmful commands under task shifts or sensor faults, where $\mathcal{C}$ is the baseline pipeline for moment-to-torque scaling, delay, filtering, and saturation.
Evaluation must cover moment fidelity, fault discrimination, and the balance between safety and utility; none of these criteria suffices alone.

\subsection{Reliability-Aware Causal TCN}

Training-participant channel means $\mathbf{c}$ and standard deviations $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ normalize each observation as $\mathbf{z}_t=(\mathbf{x}_t-\mathbf{c})\oslash\max(\mathbf{v},10^{-6})$, with $\oslash$ elementwise division.
Following \citet{molinaro2024task} and \citet{bai2018tcn}, the shared encoder has $L=5$ residual blocks, each with two causal convolutions of kernel size $k=5$ and dilation $2^{\ell-1}$, weight normalization, ReLU, and dropout.
The receptive field is $H=1+2(k-1)\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}2^{\ell-1}=249$ samples, and the first $H-1$ window outputs are excluded from training and evaluation.

Five $1\!\times\!1$ heads produce the moment mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$, log variance $\mathbf{s}_t$ (clamped to $[-8,5]$), binary fault logit $a_t$, clean-input reconstruction $\widehat{\mathbf{z}}_t$, and $h$-step forecast $\overline{\mathbf{z}}_{t+h\mid t}$, giving the diagonal predictive distribution
\begin{equation}
    p(\mathbf{y}_t\mid\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1:t})
    = \mathcal{N}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t,
      \operatorname{diag}(\exp\mathbf{s}_t)\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Fault-Augmented Multi-Task Learning}

For each training window, we sample clean data or one of six faults through an operator $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\mathcal{A}_f(\mathbf{x};\boldsymbol{\xi})$ at fixed severity and target group: the insole and encoder groups are zeroed outright; the shank IMU takes a bias ramp of $0.25$ channel standard deviations and the thigh IMU an axis-scale error of $0.35$; packet loss holds each sample independently with probability $0.15$ across all groups; the foot IMU freezes for the stuck-IMU case; and sensor delay shifts every group by $10$ samples ($50$~ms), scaled by $1.0$, $1.6$, $0.6$, $2.0$, and $1.2$ for foot, shank, thigh, insole, and encoder, so modalities become mutually asynchronous rather than uniformly late.
Each fault occupies its whole window, so the fault label $b_f$ below is window-constant.
The three held-out generators reuse the same rates by different mechanisms: burst loss drops runs of $\max(2,\operatorname{round}(2+24p))$ samples at rate $p$, partial loss draws a per-channel mask, and delay jitter redraws the shift per window in $[0.5,1.5]$ times nominal.

With $m_{jt}$ indicating a valid moment label and $b_f=\mathbb{I}[f\neq\mathrm{clean}]$, the masked heteroscedastic loss is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mom}}
&=\frac{1}{2\sum_{j,t}m_{jt}}\sum_{j,t}m_{jt}\,\ell_{jt},\\
\ell_{jt}&=s_{jt}+(y_{jt}-\mu_{jt})^2\exp(-s_{jt}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The moment head receives $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}$ with the clean target $\mathbf{y}$, encouraging robustness when a fault leaves enough information.
The fault head uses timestepwise binary cross-entropy:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fault}}=-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{t\in\Omega}
\big[&b_f\log\sigma(a_t)\\
&+(1-b_f)\log(1-\sigma(a_t))\big],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega$ contains the timesteps with at least one valid joint label.

The auxiliary heads recover the uncorrupted normalized sequence instead of reproducing the corrupted input:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}} & = \operatorname{MSE}_{\Omega}
\left(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}_t(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}),\mathbf{z}_t(\mathbf{x})\right),\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}} & = \operatorname{MSE}_{\Omega}
\left(\overline{\mathbf{z}}_{t+h\mid t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1:t}),\mathbf{z}_{t+h}(\mathbf{x})\right).
\end{align}
The complete objective is $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mom}}+\lambda_f\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fault}}+\lambda_r\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}+\lambda_p\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}$ with $(\lambda_f,\lambda_r,\lambda_p)=(0.2,0.1,0.1)$.
Reconstruction updates the shared encoder, whereas forecasting stops gradients at $\mathbf{h}_t$ so that it acts as a temporal probe.

\subsection{Complementary Integrity Signals}

Different faults affect different signal properties, so the monitor considers eight candidates. The learned ones are fault probability $q_t^{\mathrm{fault}}=\sigma(a_t)$, mean aleatoric scale $q_t^{\mathrm{ale}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_j\exp(s_{jt}/2)$, reconstruction and forecast residuals, and optional epistemic scale.
For a modality group $G$, the residual scores are
\begin{align}
q_t^{\mathrm{rec}} &= \max_G\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{c\in G}
(\widehat z_{ct}-\widetilde z_{ct})^2,\\
q_{t+h}^{\mathrm{pred}} &= \max_G\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{c\in G}
(\overline z_{c,t+h\mid t}-\widetilde z_{c,t+h})^2.
\end{align}
A forecast made at $t$ is scored only when its target arrives at $t+h$, so risk at $u$ compares $\overline{\mathbf{z}}_{u\mid u-h}$ with $\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}_u$ and the channel is zero for the first $h$ samples, preserving command-time causality. Epistemic scale is the joint-averaged standard deviation across stochastic-dropout passes or ensemble members.

Three causal statistics cover signatures that learned heads may miss. Staleness is the moving fraction of nearly unchanged channels, and coherence is one minus the mean absolute rolling correlation between differenced, corresponding IMU axes; like staleness it uses a trailing window, so its value at $t$ needs no future sample. Drift is the largest modality-averaged channel slope, fitted over the whole window and therefore the only genuinely window-level channel.
The gate we evaluate consumes staleness but not coherence or drift, so both appear as diagnostic channels: for drift this is forced by its definition, whereas for coherence it is an implementation choice of the evaluated system, and the severity analysis below quantifies its cost. Of the two, only coherence is ever selected.

\subsection{Validation-Calibrated Fault Detection}

For validation window $i$, each channel is averaged over its valid timesteps $\Omega_i$ to obtain $Q_{ik}=|\Omega_i|^{-1}\sum_{t\in\Omega_i}q_{it}^{k}$. The detector reference is $\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}}=\operatorname{Quantile}_{0.9}(\{Q_{ik}:i\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{clean}}\})$ over clean validation windows, and a subset $S$ fuses as $R_i^{\mathrm{det}}(S)=\max_{k\in S}Q_{ik}/\max(\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}},10^{-6})$.
Using validation faults only, we enumerate subsets of at most three channels and, given their validation-fault AUROCs $A_f(S)$, maximize
\begin{equation}
    J_{\mathrm{det}}(S)=\operatorname{mean}_f A_f(S)
    \!+\!0.25\min_f A_f(S)\!-\!0.002(|S|\!-\!1),
\end{equation}
where the second term rewards the hardest fault and the last favors smaller subsets.
Both $S$ and $\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}}$ are frozen before test evaluation, and burst loss, partial-channel loss, and delay jitter are excluded from training and detector selection alike.

Coherence and drift sit outside the risk fusion the evaluated gate computes, so one fused number would overstate what the deployed system sees.
We therefore run the same selection twice on the same validation scores: \emph{Detector-all} searches all eight candidates and bounds offline diagnosis, while \emph{Detector-online} restricts the pool to the command-time channels $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}$ defined below, and is the operative number for every deployed-detection claim including the abstract.

\subsection{Utility-Aware Assistance Gate}

The command-time risk uses the causal timestep-level channels
$\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}=\{\mathrm{fault},\mathrm{ale},\mathrm{rec},\mathrm{pred},\mathrm{epi},\mathrm{stale}\}$ that are available in a given configuration:
\begin{equation}
    r_t=\max_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}}
    q_t^k/\max(\rho_k^{\mathrm{gate}},10^{-6}).
\end{equation}
We share the detector scale, $\rho_k^{\mathrm{gate}}:=\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}}$, to keep window- and timestep-level scores comparable; it is not a timestep-score quantile. Clean held-out-task validation trials and designated validation faults instead calibrate the deadband $d$ and softness $\gamma$.

The risk is converted into a continuous gain:
\begin{equation}
g_t = \operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(
1-\frac{r_t-(1+d)}{\gamma}\right),
\end{equation}
so $g_t=1$ within the nominal region $r_t\leq1+d$ and decreases linearly beyond it.
Starting from $d_0=0.1$, we set
\begin{equation}
    d=\min\!\left(d_{\max},\max\left[d_0,
    \operatorname{Quantile}_{0.9}(r^{\mathrm{val\text{-}OOD}})-1\right]\right),
\end{equation}
with $d_{\max}=2$, using clean held-out-task trials from validation participants.
We then search $\gamma\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2,4,8\}$.
Feasible values retain at least 95\% of clean aligned torque, meet the clean wrong-direction tolerance, and do not increase wrong-direction torque under validation faults.
Writing $\kappa_{\mathrm{clean}}$ for gated-over-ungated clean retention and, over validation faults, $\bar\delta$ for the relative wrong-direction reduction $\min(1,\max(0,1-w^{\mathrm{gated}}/w^{\mathrm{ungated}}))$ and $\bar\kappa$ for fault retention, feasible pairs are ranked by
\begin{equation}
    J_{\mathrm{gate}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{clean}} + 2\bar\delta + 0.25\,\bar\kappa,
\end{equation}
so safety dominates but is not bought by closing the gate outright.
The baseline transformation $\mathcal{C}$ scales the estimated hip and knee moments by $0.20$ and $0.15$, applies the configured joint delays and a 6-Hz low-pass filter, and clips torque to $0.45$ Nm/kg; the gated command $\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\mathrm{cmd}}_t=g_t\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1:t})$ attenuates unreliable estimates but does not replace independent actuator saturation or hardware limits.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Data, Splits, and Comparisons}

We use the released exoskeleton sensor and biological-moment data underlying the task-agnostic controller \citep{molinaro2024task,scherpereel2023dataset}, resampled to a uniform $200$~Hz, so one sample is $5$~ms and the forecast horizon $h=1$ is one control step.
Inputs comprise $25$ unilateral channels in five modality groups $G$: foot, shank, and thigh IMU ($6$ channels each, three gyroscope and three accelerometer axes), insole ($3$), and hip--knee encoders ($4$), with the action profiles of the input ablation adding a sixth group; targets are right hip and knee moments in Nm/kg.
Of the $15$ participants, $11$ train, $2$ validate, and $2$ test, and jumping, cutting, weight lifting, and lunges are reserved as task OOD; no test participant informs preprocessing, calibration, detector selection, or gate tuning. The leave-one-subject-out analysis rotates over all $15$, each fold taking the preceding participant as validation, and the primary baseline is a deterministic, non-augmented TCN on the same split.
Ablations add fault augmentation, probabilistic regression, reconstruction, and forecasting in order, with additional dropout, ensemble, action-input, severity, and leave-one-subject-out analyses.

\subsection{Metrics and Implementation}

We report RMSE, MAE, $R^2$, Gaussian NLL, uncertainty--error correlation, interval coverage and calibration error, and per-channel and fused fault AUROC. Control-replay metrics are defined against the ideal command $\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\star}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{y})$, the \emph{true} moment through the same transformation $\mathcal{C}$.
A joint-timestep pair $n$ is \emph{active} when its label is valid and $|\tau^{\star}_n|>0.02$ Nm/kg; the set $\mathcal{A}$ depends only on the true moment, so it is identical for gated and ungated commands and cannot be shrunk by attenuation.
A step count is blind to magnitude, rating one large reversal below a hundred negligible ones at opposite physical risk, so we pair it with a wrong-direction energy.
Writing $u_n=\tau^{\mathrm{cmd}}_n$ and $\Delta t=1/200$~s, the ratio $w$, retained aligned torque $\kappa$, and energy $E_{\mathrm{wrong}}$ are
\begin{align}
w &= \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\textstyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}}
     \mathbb{I}[u_n\tau^{\star}_n<0],\\
\kappa &= \frac{\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}}\max(0,\,u_n\operatorname{sgn}\tau^{\star}_n)}
       {\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}}|\tau^{\star}_n|},\\
E_{\mathrm{wrong}} &= \textstyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}}
       \max(0,\,-u_n\tau^{\star}_n)\,\Delta t,
\end{align}
reported with the peak $|u_n|$ over wrong-direction steps and the mean absolute command jerk.
Since $u_n=g_t\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\mu})_n$ with $g_t\geq0$, scaling never flips a sign: every reduction in $w$ comes from steps where $g_t$ reaches exactly zero, and $\kappa$ is the price paid for them, so both must be reported.
A first-order actuator proxy also estimates net-moment RMS and work-related quantities, as relative offline comparisons rather than metabolic outcomes.
The model has five 80-channel TCN blocks, kernel size 5, dropout 0.15, and 768-sample windows at stride 384 with the first 248 outputs masked, trained for 20 epochs with Adam ($10^{-3}$) under seeds 7, 13, and 23; we report mean, standard deviation, and paired seed-level differences.
Three seeds quantify only training stochasticity, so the interval for the main safety claim instead comes from the fifteen leave-one-subject-out folds, as a percentile bootstrap $95\%$ interval over folds ($10{,}000$ resamples, fixed seed) on the per-subject wrong-direction reduction.
Over ten scenarios, two splits, and several metrics, an uncorrected test family would be uninterpretable and a corrected one underpowered at three seeds, so we report no per-scenario tests: those entries are descriptive and the confirmatory claim rests on that pre-specified between-subject interval.

\subsection{Fault Detection under Task Shift}

Figure~\ref{fig:detection-taxonomy} reports per-fault AUROC for both frozen fusions and their candidate channels. Across the six training-time faults, Detector-online reaches macro-AUROC $0.815\pm0.006$ on ID and $0.740\pm0.002$ on held-out tasks, against $0.849\pm0.005$ and $0.773\pm0.002$ for Detector-all; for the unseen burst, partial-loss, and delay-jitter generators the pairs are $0.812\pm0.011$ and $0.715\pm0.002$ against $0.842\pm0.010$ and $0.778\pm0.004$.
The gap is concentrated rather than uniform: validation selects $\{$residual, staleness, coherence$\}$ for Detector-all and $\{$residual, staleness$\}$ for Detector-online, and losing coherence costs fixed sensor delay $0.878\to0.628$ on ID and $0.774\to0.557$ on OOD tasks, with delay jitter behaving the same way ($0.867\to0.641$, $0.766\to0.559$).
Elsewhere the smaller subset is no worse, and burst packet loss improves from $0.743$ to $0.886$ on ID tasks because coherence is inverted there rather than uninformative: at $0.055$ AUROC its clean-side scores sit above its faulty ones, and a maximum-fusion inherits that raised clean baseline.
Averaged over all nine faults the strongest single channel is staleness ($0.785$ on ID and $0.796$ on OOD tasks); coherence is the most extreme rather than the broadest, reaching $1.000$ for partial channel loss and a stuck IMU but only $0.658$ and $0.663$ on average, while IMU bias stays the hardest fused case at $0.502$ and $0.504$, close to chance and so nearly indistinguishable from valid motion with these sensors.

On clean ID and OOD trials the gate retains $0.960$ and $0.961$ of aligned assistance at wrong-direction changes of $-0.0005$ and $-0.0074$; across training-time faults the ratio changes by $-0.0052$ on ID and $-0.0109$ on OOD tasks, and for unseen generators by $-0.0017$ and $-0.0103$.
Mean gate values stay between $0.904$ and $0.978$: since only $g_t=0$ can change $w$, the reduction is carried by a sparse set of fully closed steps rather than a broadly suppressed command, and the retention figures bound what the intermediate attenuation costs.

The energy view shows the gate is not merely trimming counted steps. On held-out tasks under training-time faults, $E_{\mathrm{wrong}}$ falls from $0.0439\pm0.0062$ to $0.0298\pm0.0030$ Nm$\cdot$s/kg, a $32\%$ reduction where $w$ falls only $11\%$; for unseen generators, $0.0412\pm0.0066$ to $0.0270\pm0.0041$; and the peak wrong-direction command drops with it, $0.175\pm0.025$ to $0.137\pm0.017$ Nm/kg on held-out seen faults, so the closed steps are the large reversals rather than a uniform sample.
The effect is smaller on ID tasks ($0.0581\to0.0469$ under seen faults) and negligible on clean ID trials ($0.0408\to0.0400$), as intended, but clean held-out trials fall $0.0316\to0.0236$: a $25\%$ reduction of the same order as the faulty case, so the gate also acts on ordinary task-shift error, which we revisit in the limitations.
The cost appears in smoothness: mean absolute command jerk rises from $0.237$ to $0.279$ on held-out seen faults and $0.249$ to $0.318$ for unseen generators, because a time-varying gain modulates an otherwise smooth command, a trade-off a fixed-gain policy cannot express.

\section{Discussion}
Reliability estimates matter only if they change the command rather than serving as confidence scores.
The validation-calibrated gate reduces wrong-direction assistance under non-clean conditions by about $0.007$ absolute while retaining $96.1\%$ of clean aligned torque: a modest improvement at one operating point, obtained without turning assistance off.
Since AUROC does not measure control consequences, both wrong-direction torque and retained assistance are needed to expose that trade-off, which the ablation shows move independently.

Fault mechanisms violate different signal properties: reconstruction is most informative for a missing insole on ID tasks, staleness for packet loss and for the insole under task shift, and coherence for partial channel loss and delay, so fusion covers more cases than any single channel.
Some auxiliary heads nonetheless reduce the aggregate well outside seed noise, and clean RMSE is worse than the split-matched deterministic TCN, so predictive uncertainty and auxiliary training are not universal solutions.
The layer complements the two baseline directions, which expand behavioral coverage \citep{molinaro2024task} and reduce training-data demands \citep{scherpereel2025domain}: a domain-adapted estimator could reuse the monitor, but its calibration would have to be repeated in the target sensor domain, and devices with different actuator authority should pick their own point on the validation trade-off.


\section{Limitations}
The reported reduction is not attributable to fault detection alone.
On clean held-out-task trials, with no fault present, gating still lowers wrong-direction torque by $0.74$ percentage points, the same order as the $1.09$ and $1.03$ points on faulty held-out trials, and the energy view agrees at $25\%$ against $32\%$ and $34\%$.
The risk channels respond to prediction error in general, so an unknown part of the aggregate effect suppresses ordinary task-shift error rather than sensor failure; separating the two needs a fault-free OOD control condition our protocol lacks.
The evaluated faults are synthetic and cannot represent the whole hardware failure distribution; we did not run the gate online on a physical exoskeleton, and the actuator replay assumes fixed recorded kinematics, supporting relative comparisons rather than claims about interaction safety, human adaptation, or metabolic benefit.
Labels and sensing come from one exoskeleton platform, and we do not jointly train the domain-adaptation model of \citet{scherpereel2025domain}.
A gate reduces exposure to a detected failure but cannot guarantee safety under an undetectable or adversarial fault, so independent low-level torque and hardware limits remain necessary.

\section{Ethical Statement}

Reliability-aware assistance may reduce risk from silent sensing failures, but overconfident claims could encourage premature deployment around human users, so we distinguish offline replay from human-subject evidence, report negative and weak detector results, and do not present the gate as a safety guarantee. Released participant data must be handled under the source dataset's consent conditions.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a reliability layer that combines learned outputs with causal integrity signals and uses validation-calibrated risk to attenuate moment-based assistance, evaluated across participant, task, and sensor-fault shifts through both the harmful torque removed and the assistance lost in exchange. Future work targets the weak detection of slow bias and real-time validation with human participants.
