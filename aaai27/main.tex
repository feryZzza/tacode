\begin{abstract}
Powered lower-limb exoskeletons must decide how much to assist as the wearer moves freely; task-agnostic controllers do so by mapping wearable-sensor histories to biological joint moments. Because that mapping is continuous and uses no task label, a sensor fault propagates into commanded torque even when nominal error is low. Clean accuracy cannot expose such isolated events, predictive uncertainty alone need not flag a held but locally plausible value, and detecting an anomaly still does not say what the actuator should do. We formulate deployment reliability as a validation-calibrated risk--utility decision: a causal monitor fuses probabilistic moment estimation, learned consistency signals, and mechanism-specific integrity checks into a continuous torque gain, with normalization, detector selection, and gate tuning fixed on validation data alone. Under participant-disjoint replay over in-distribution and held-out tasks, six training-time faults, and three fault generators withheld from training and detector selection, reliability training costs $0.011$ and $0.021$ Nm/kg in clean RMSE; gating then cuts opposition-weighted wrong-direction torque exposure by $0.0070\pm0.0015$ $(\mathrm{Nm/kg})^2\mathrm{s}$ beyond the matched clean effect and wrong-direction timesteps by $0.74$ percentage points, keeping $96.1\%$ of the ungated clean aligned command. Torque rate nevertheless rises by $18\%$--$28\%$, retention-matched random attenuation is indistinguishable in $32$ of $60$ cells, and sub-window faults are mostly missed. The benefit is thus one of attenuation magnitude at a conservative offline operating point, not of selective timing or online safety. Deployment reliability must therefore be judged jointly through fault discrimination, control consequences, retained utility, and matched controls, which no single metric separates.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}
Powered lower-limb exoskeletons can reduce locomotor effort and support impaired gait, but controllers must decide what assistance is appropriate as the user moves among activities \citep{young2017state,kim2019exosuit,awad2017soft,siviy2023opportunities}.
Mode-based controllers decide from a fixed task set \citep{camargo2021classification,kang2022classification}, which scales poorly to transitions and unstructured motion; task-agnostic control instead estimates biological hip and knee moments from wearable sensors and uses that continuous state to assist cyclic, transient, and unstructured movements \citep{molinaro2022hip,molinaro2024unifies,molinaro2024task}.

Wider task coverage does not ensure that every deployed prediction is reliable.
Frozen streams, drift, burst packet loss, or asynchronous modalities can corrupt an otherwise familiar trial, and because predicted moments feed the torque pipeline, such errors may remove useful assistance or reverse its direction.
Clean RMSE cannot reveal these isolated events, predictive uncertainty alone need not detect a held but locally plausible value or slow drift \citep{ovadia2019trust}, and detecting an anomaly still does not specify what the actuator should do.
The question is whether such an estimator can detect several forms of sensor failure and respond without suppressing valid assistance.

We address it with a causal multi-head TCN trained on synthetic faults, predicting joint moments, data-dependent uncertainty, fault probability, clean-input reconstruction, and short-horizon sensor values, and a separate monitor that fuses these with causal integrity checks into a continuous torque gate. \section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Task-agnostic exoskeleton control.}
Conventional controllers infer a discrete locomotion mode or gait phase before applying a task-specific torque policy \citep{camargo2021classification,kang2022classification}.
Moment-based control instead uses an instantaneous biological joint-moment estimate as a continuous control state, first for subject-independent hip assistance \citep{shepherd2022deep,molinaro2022hip}, then across ambulatory conditions \citep{molinaro2024unifies}, and finally for coordinated hip and knee assistance without task classification \citep{molinaro2024task}, whose sensing, target, and control transformation we adopt. Those studies evaluate nominal accuracy; their sensor ablations measure sensitivity to removing a modality but do not estimate sensor health or change assistance when a fault is detected.

\paragraph{Data efficiency and domain shift.}
Because moment labels need motion-capture and force-plate measurement and are device-specific, \citet{scherpereel2025domain} translate open-source biomechanics data into a target sensor domain by CycleGAN; personalization \citep{zhang2017humanloop,slade2022personalizing} and simulation-trained policies \citep{luo2024simulation} likewise act before deployment. All change how the estimator is obtained; we address corrupted observations met afterwards on-device.

\paragraph{Uncertainty, anomalies, and selective prediction.}
Heteroscedastic regression separates input-dependent noise from the predicted mean \citep{kendall2017uncertainties}, while MC dropout and deep ensembles approximate epistemic uncertainty \citep{gal2016dropout,lakshminarayanan2017ensembles}, though both may become miscalibrated under shift \citep{ovadia2019trust}.
Uncertainty-aware ankle assistance disengages on OOD estimates, targeting behavioral novelty rather than structured sensor faults \citep{tourk2025uncertainty}. In multivariate time series, reconstruction error and changes in inter-variable relationships supply evidence that predictive variance misses \citep{song2023memto,dai2024sarad}, motivating mechanism-specific channels. Selective prediction abstains when expected error is high \citep{geifman2019selectivenet}; an assistive controller cannot abstain without saying what the actuator should do, so we map validation-normalized evidence to a continuous gain and evaluate the command trade-offs.

\paragraph{Fault-tolerant exoskeleton control.}
Prior exoskeleton studies inject freeze, stuck-at-zero, and bit-flip faults into dynamic models to find critical exposure durations \citep{fabarisov2020fault}, or use model-based observers to reconstruct sensor and actuator faults before trajectory tracking \citep{mokhtari2022fault,sun2025reconstruction}, exploiting an explicit plant model. We instead monitor a learned, task-agnostic moment estimator from heterogeneous wearable observations; it detects but does not reconstruct faults, so its offline replay complements rather than replaces plant-level fault isolation and certified hardware limits.

\section{Problem Formulation}

Let $\mathbf{x}_{1:T}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times T}$ denote a causal input window of unilateral measurements from the foot, shank, and thigh IMUs, the insole, and the hip and knee encoders, and let the target $\mathbf{y}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$ hold mass-normalized biological hip and knee moments.
At deployment an unknown corruption $f\in\mathcal{F}$ may transform the observations into $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1:T}$.
We seek a predictor $\widehat{\mathbf{y}}_t$ and a reliability gate $g_t\in[0,1]$ such that the commanded torque
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\tau}^{\mathrm{cmd}}_t
    = g_t\,\mathcal{C}(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}_{1:t})
\end{equation}
preserves useful assistance for clean inputs while suppressing harmful commands under task shift or sensor faults, where $\mathcal{C}$ is the baseline pipeline for moment-to-torque scaling, delay, filtering, and saturation.
Evaluation must cover moment fidelity, fault discrimination, command attenuation, retained assistance, and smoothness; no one criterion suffices.

\subsection{Reliability-Aware Causal TCN}

Training-participant channel means $\mathbf{c}$ and standard deviations $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ normalize each observation as $\mathbf{z}_t=(\mathbf{x}_t-\mathbf{c})\oslash\max(\mathbf{v},10^{-6})$, with $\oslash$ elementwise division.
Following \citet{molinaro2024task} and \citet{bai2018tcn}, the shared encoder has $L=5$ residual blocks, each with two causal convolutions of kernel size $k=5$ and dilation $2^{\ell-1}$, weight normalization, ReLU, and dropout.
The receptive field is $H=1+2(k-1)\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}2^{\ell-1}=249$ samples; the first $H-1$ window outputs are excluded from training and evaluation.
Five $1\!\times\!1$ heads produce the moment mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$, log variance $\mathbf{s}_t$ (clamped to $[-8,5]$), binary fault logit $a_t$, clean-input reconstruction $\widehat{\mathbf{z}}_t$, and $h$-step forecast $\overline{\mathbf{z}}_{t+h\mid t}$, giving the diagonal predictive distribution $p(\mathbf{y}_t\mid\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1:t})=\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t,\operatorname{diag}(\exp\mathbf{s}_t))$.

\subsection{Fault-Augmented Multi-Task Learning}

For each training window we sample clean data or one of six faults through an operator $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\mathcal{A}_f(\mathbf{x};\boldsymbol{\xi})$ at fixed severity and target group: the insole or encoder group is zeroed; the shank IMU takes a bias ramp of $0.25$ channel standard deviations; packet loss holds each sample independently with probability $0.15$ across all groups; the foot IMU freezes; or sensor delay shifts every group by $10$ samples ($50$~ms), scaled by $1.0$, $1.6$, $0.6$, $2.0$, and $1.2$ for foot, shank, thigh, insole, and encoder, so modalities become mutually asynchronous rather than uniformly late.
Each fault occupies its whole window, so the fault label $b_f$ below is window-constant.
The three held-out operators reuse known fault families at the same nominal rates: burst loss forward-fills runs of length $\max(2,\operatorname{round}(2+24p))$ with per-timestep start probability $p$ divided by that length, partial loss draws an independent channel--time mask, and delay jitter redraws each modality's shift per window in $[0.5,1.5]$ times nominal.
They test operator transfer within known packet-loss and delay semantics, not open-set generalization; the supplement lists every protocol role.

With $m_{jt}$ indicating a valid moment label and $b_f=\mathbb{I}[f\neq\mathrm{clean}]$, the masked heteroscedastic loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mom}}
=\frac{\sum_{j,t}m_{jt}\left[s_{jt}+(y_{jt}-\mu_{jt})^2\exp(-s_{jt})\right]}{2\sum_{j,t}m_{jt}}.
\end{equation}
The moment head receives $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}$ with the clean target $\mathbf{y}$, encouraging robustness when a fault leaves enough information, and the fault head uses timestepwise binary cross-entropy $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fault}}$ between $b_f$ and $\sigma(a_t)$, averaged over the set $\Omega$ of timesteps with at least one valid joint label.
The auxiliary heads recover the uncorrupted normalized sequence rather than the corrupted input, as $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}=\operatorname{MSE}_{\Omega}(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}_t(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}),\mathbf{z}_t(\mathbf{x}))$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}=\operatorname{MSE}_{\Omega}(\overline{\mathbf{z}}_{t+h\mid t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1:t}),\mathbf{z}_{t+h}(\mathbf{x}))$.
The objective is $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mom}}+\lambda_f\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fault}}+\lambda_r\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}+\lambda_p\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}$ with $(\lambda_f,\lambda_r,\lambda_p)=(0.2,0.1,0.1)$.
Reconstruction updates the shared encoder, whereas forecasting stops gradients at $\mathbf{h}_t$ so it acts as a temporal probe.

\subsection{Complementary Integrity Signals}

Different faults affect different signal properties, so the monitor considers eight candidates. The learned ones are fault probability $q_t^{\mathrm{fault}}=\sigma(a_t)$, mean aleatoric scale $q_t^{\mathrm{ale}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_j\exp(s_{jt}/2)$, reconstruction and forecast residuals, and an optional epistemic scale.
Over modality groups $G$, the residual scores are $q_t^{\mathrm{rec}}=\max_G|G|^{-1}\sum_{c\in G}(\widehat z_{ct}-\widetilde z_{ct})^2$ and $q_{t+h}^{\mathrm{pred}}=\max_G|G|^{-1}\sum_{c\in G}(\overline z_{c,t+h\mid t}-\widetilde z_{c,t+h})^2$.
A forecast made at $t$ is scored only when its target arrives at $t+h$, so the score at $u$ compares $\overline{\mathbf{z}}_{u\mid u-h}$ with $\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}_u$ and the channel is zero for the first $h$ samples, preserving command-time causality. Epistemic scale is the joint-averaged standard deviation across dropout passes or ensemble members.

Three integrity statistics cover signatures the learned heads may miss. Staleness is the moving fraction of nearly unchanged channels and coherence is one minus the mean absolute rolling correlation between differenced, corresponding IMU axes; both use trailing windows, so their value at $t$ needs no future sample. Drift is the largest modality-averaged channel slope, fitted over the whole window and therefore the only noncausal, window-level channel.
The evaluated gate consumes staleness but not coherence or drift, so both are diagnostic: for drift this is forced by its definition, for coherence it is an implementation choice whose cost the severity analysis quantifies. Of the two, only coherence is ever selected.

\subsection{Validation-Normalized Fault Evidence}

For validation window $i$, each channel is averaged over its valid timesteps $\Omega_i$ to obtain $Q_{ik}=|\Omega_i|^{-1}\sum_{t\in\Omega_i}q_{it}^{k}$. The detector reference is $\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}}=\operatorname{Quantile}_{0.9}(\{Q_{ik}:i\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{clean}}\})$ over clean validation windows, and a subset $S$ fuses as $R_i^{\mathrm{det}}(S)=\max_{k\in S}Q_{ik}/\max(\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}},10^{-6})$.
Using five validation faults (insole missing, encoder dropout, IMU bias, packet loss, and sensor delay), we enumerate subsets of at most three channels and, given their validation-fault AUROCs $A_f(S)$, maximize
\begin{equation}
    J_{\mathrm{det}}(S)=\operatorname{mean}_f A_f(S)
    \!+\!0.25\min_f A_f(S)\!-\!0.002(|S|\!-\!1),
\end{equation}
where the second term rewards the hardest fault and the last favors smaller subsets.
Both $S$ and $\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}}$ are frozen before test evaluation, and burst loss, partial-channel loss, and delay jitter are excluded from training and detector selection alike.

Coherence and drift sit outside the evaluated gate, so one fused number would overstate what deployment has available.
We run the same subset search twice on the same validation scores: \emph{Detector-all} searches all eight candidates as an offline diagnostic reference, while \emph{Detector-gate} restricts the pool to the channels implemented in $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}$ below. These subsets serve only window-level AUROC evaluation.
The gate does not reuse the selected subset: it fuses all available members of $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}$ and tunes the actuation mapping separately.

\subsection{Validation-Selected Assistance Gate}

The command-time anomaly score uses the causal timestep-level channels
$\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}=\{\mathrm{fault},\mathrm{ale},\mathrm{rec},\mathrm{pred},\mathrm{epi},\mathrm{stale}\}$ that are available in a given configuration, fused as $r_t=\max_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}}q_t^k/\max(\rho_k^{\mathrm{gate}},10^{-6})$.
We share the detector scale, $\rho_k^{\mathrm{gate}}:=\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}}$, to place window- and timestep-level scores on one numerical scale; it is a quantile of window means, not a timestep quantile or a calibrated posterior. Clean held-out-task validation trials and designated validation faults select the deadband $d$ and softness $\gamma$.

The score becomes a raw gain $\widetilde g_t=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}(1-[r_t-(1+d)]/\gamma)$, so $\widetilde g_t=1$ within the nominal region $r_t\leq1+d$ and decreases linearly beyond it.
The deadband is $d=\min(2,\max[0.1,\operatorname{Quantile}_{0.9}(r^{\mathrm{val\text{-}held}})-1])$ on clean held-out-task trials from validation participants, and we search $\gamma\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2,4,8\}$.
The archived evaluation required at least 95\% clean aligned-command retention and recorded non-increase checks on the clean wrong-direction fraction and four validation faults; candidates were ranked by $\kappa_{\mathrm{clean}}+2\bar\delta+0.25\bar\kappa$, with $\kappa_{\mathrm{clean}}$ the gated-over-ungated clean retention and $\bar\delta,\bar\kappa$ the mean relative wrong-direction reduction and retention ratio over validation faults.
That describes the archived point, but its non-increase checks are not substantive constraints: because $g_t\geq0$ the gate cannot create a sign reversal, so the only active feasibility condition is clean retention and $\bar\delta$ merely ranks hard closures.
We therefore reselect on validation data, maximizing a score whose primary safety term is the relative $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ reduction over validation faults, under six clean-side constraints---aligned-command and capped-overlap retention, tracking RMSE, full-shutdown fraction, mean absolute torque rate, and false shutdowns per minute---searching $\gamma$ jointly with the causal rate limits $(\lambda^{\downarrow},\lambda^{\uparrow})$ over $216$ candidates per seed. The supplement gives the exact score and levels.
Unlike wrong-direction non-increase, none of these six is guaranteed by nonnegative gating.
The rate limiter is causal: from $\widetilde g_t$ it applies the bounds $g_{t-1}-\lambda^{\downarrow}$ and $g_{t-1}+\lambda^{\uparrow}$ only when the corresponding limit is positive; a grid value of zero leaves that direction unrestricted. It neither anticipates onsets nor changes what the gate sees.
The baseline transformation $\mathcal{C}$ scales the estimated hip and knee moments by $0.20$ and $0.15$, applies the configured joint delays and a 6-Hz low-pass filter, and clips torque to $0.45$ Nm/kg; the gated command $\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\mathrm{cmd}}_t=g_t\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1:t})$ attenuates unreliable estimates but does not replace independent actuator saturation or hardware limits.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Data, Splits, and Comparisons}

We use the released exoskeleton sensor and biological-moment data underlying the task-agnostic controller \citep{molinaro2024task,scherpereel2023dataset}, resampled to a uniform $200$~Hz, so one sample is $5$~ms and the forecast horizon $h=1$ is one control step.
Inputs comprise $25$ unilateral channels in five modality groups $G$: foot, shank, and thigh IMU ($6$ each, three gyroscope and three accelerometer axes), insole ($3$), and hip--knee encoders ($4$), with the action profiles of the input ablation adding a sixth group; targets are right hip and knee moments in Nm/kg.
Of the $15$ participants, $11$ train, $2$ validate, and $2$ test. Jumping, cutting, weight lifting, and lunges are excluded from estimator fitting and form the training-held-out task set, written HT below against ID for in-distribution tasks; clean trials from these categories tune the gate on validation participants only, so they are not unseen to the full pipeline. No test participant informs preprocessing, score normalization, detector selection, or gate tuning. The leave-one-subject-out analysis rotates over all $15$, each fold taking the preceding participant as validation; the baseline is a deterministic, non-augmented TCN on the same split, and ablations add fault augmentation, probabilistic regression, reconstruction, and forecasting in order.

\subsection{Metrics and Implementation}

We report RMSE, MAE, $R^2$, Gaussian NLL, uncertainty--error correlation, interval coverage and calibration error, and per-channel and fused fault AUROC. Control-replay metrics are defined against the ideal command $\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\star}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{y})$, the \emph{true} moment through the same $\mathcal{C}$.
A joint-timestep pair $n$ is \emph{active} when both joint labels at its timestep are valid and $|\tau^{\star}_n|>0.02$ Nm/kg; the set $\mathcal{A}$ depends only on the true moment, so it is identical for gated and ungated commands and cannot be shrunk by attenuation.
Writing $u_n=\tau^{\mathrm{cmd}}_n$, our primary command-consequence metric is the opposition-weighted torque-product integral
\begin{equation}
X_{\mathrm{opp}}(u)=\textstyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}}
\max(0,-u_n\tau^{\star}_n)\,\Delta t,\quad \Delta t=1/200\ {\rm s},
\end{equation}
in $(\mathrm{Nm/kg})^2\mathrm{s}$; because no angular velocity appears it is an exposure surrogate, not mechanical energy.
We also report the wrong-direction fraction
$w(u)=|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{I}[u_n\tau_n^\star<0]$, peak $|u_n|$ over those pairs, mean absolute torque rate $|\Delta u|/\Delta t$ in Nm\,kg$^{-1}$\,s$^{-1}$, and command retention $\kappa=\sum_{\mathcal{A}}\max(0,u_n^{\mathrm{gated}}\operatorname{sgn}\tau_n^\star)/\sum_{\mathcal{A}}\max(0,u_n^{\mathrm{ungated}}\operatorname{sgn}\tau_n^\star)$, a weighted fraction of the baseline's same-direction command, not a tracking measure.
For the clean-side constraint, capped-overlap adequacy replaces each same-direction magnitude by $\min(|u_n|,|\tau_n^\star|)$ and normalizes by $\sum_{\mathcal A}|\tau_n^\star|$; capped-overlap retention is its gated-over-ungated ratio, so over-assistance is not rewarded.
Since $u_n^{\mathrm{gated}}=g_nu_n^{\mathrm{ungated}}$ with $g_n\geq0$, the wrong-direction indicator factorizes into $\mathbb{I}[u_n^{\mathrm{ungated}}\tau_n^\star<0]\,\mathbb{I}[g_n>0]$, so $w(u^{\mathrm{gated}})\leq w(u^{\mathrm{ungated}})$ and $w$ responds only to exact shutdowns ($g_n=0$). Likewise $X_{\mathrm{opp}}(gu)\leq X_{\mathrm{opp}}(u)$, but it responds continuously to every attenuation $0\leq g_n<1$; retention and matched controls are thus needed to separate selective timing from blanket suppression.
A first-order actuator proxy also estimates net-moment RMS and work-related quantities, as relative offline comparisons only.
The model has five 80-channel TCN blocks, kernel size 5, dropout 0.15, and 768-sample windows at stride 384 with the first 248 outputs masked, trained for 20 epochs with AdamW ($10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) under seeds 7, 13, and 23; Gaussian NLL omits the additive $\frac12\log(2\pi)$ constant. We report mean, standard deviation, and paired seed-level differences.
Three seeds quantify training stochasticity, not participant uncertainty. For descriptive subject transfer, we summarize the fifteen leave-one-subject-out fold effects with a percentile-bootstrap $95\%$ interval over folds ($10{,}000$ resamples, fixed seed) on each subject's reduction macro-averaged over nine faults and both splits; overlapping training sets and the non-increase identity preclude a population-level reading.
Over ten scenarios, two splits, and several metrics an uncorrected test family would be uninterpretable and a corrected one underpowered at three seeds, so per-scenario entries and this fold interval are descriptive, not confirmatory.

\subsection{Fault Detection under Task Shift}

Figure~\ref{fig:detection-taxonomy} reports all nine operators and eight candidate channels. Across the six training-time faults, Detector-gate reaches macro-AUROC $0.815\pm0.006$ on ID and $0.740\pm0.002$ on HT tasks, against $0.849\pm0.005$ and $0.773\pm0.002$ for Detector-all; for the three held-out operators the pairs are $0.812\pm0.011$ and $0.715\pm0.002$ against $0.842\pm0.010$ and $0.778\pm0.004$.
The gap is concentrated, not uniform: validation selects $\{$reconstruction residual, staleness, coherence$\}$ for Detector-all and $\{$reconstruction residual, staleness$\}$ for Detector-gate, and losing coherence costs fixed sensor delay $0.878\to0.628$ on ID and $0.774\to0.557$ on HT tasks, with delay jitter similar ($0.867\to0.641$, $0.766\to0.559$).
Coherence also reverses direction for held-out burst loss (AUROC $0.055$ ID, $0.054$ HT): its clean scores then sit above its faulty ones and max fusion inherits that raised baseline, so dropping it improves ID burst loss from $0.743$ to $0.886$, exposing the high-score-is-anomalous assumption of max fusion rather than supporting an open-set claim.
Over all nine faults, staleness is the strongest single channel ($0.785$ ID, $0.796$ HT); coherence is the most extreme rather than the broadest, reaching $1.000$ for partial channel loss and a stuck IMU but $0.658$ and $0.663$ on average, while IMU bias stays the hardest fused case at $0.502$ and $0.504$, near chance.

\subsection{Control Consequences, Retention, and Smoothness}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_safety_utility.pdf}
\caption{Control consequences of the frozen gate; means over three seeds, horizontal bars cross-seed standard deviations. (a) $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ before and after gating, absolute changes labelled; (b) aligned-command retention relative to ungated, dashed line at the validation floor, filled triangles faults and open circles clean; (c) mean absolute torque rate with relative increases. ``Held-op.'' denotes the three held-out operators.}
\label{fig:safety-utility}

Figure~\ref{fig:safety-utility} makes $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$, not the hard-shutdown-sensitive count $w$, the primary consequence view.
On HT tasks $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ falls from $0.0439\pm0.0062$ to $0.0298\pm0.0030$ $(\mathrm{Nm/kg})^2\mathrm{s}$ under training-time faults and from $0.0412\pm0.0066$ to $0.0270\pm0.0041$ for held-out operators, with peak wrong-direction torque dropping from $0.175$ to $0.137$ Nm/kg.
The effect is smaller on ID faults ($0.0581\to0.0469$), negligible on clean ID trials ($0.0408\to0.0400$), and substantial on clean HT trials ($0.0316\to0.0236$): the gate also attenuates ordinary task-shift error.
Subtracting the clean gate change within each seed and split before macro-averaging the nine matched scenarios, the fault-side change of $-0.0114\pm0.0020$ against a matched clean change of $-0.0044\pm0.0009$ leaves a clean-adjusted contrast of $-0.0070\pm0.0015$; for $w$ the three values are $-0.736$, $-0.397$, and $-0.339\pm0.098$ percentage points, so only about $46\%$ of the count reduction lies beyond the clean effect.

The attenuation has two costs. Clean aligned-command retention is $0.960$ ID and $0.961$ HT but falls to $0.829$--$0.948$ under faults, and mean absolute torque rate rises from $0.237$ to $0.279$ Nm\,kg$^{-1}$\,s$^{-1}$ on HT seen faults and $0.249$ to $0.318$ for held-out operators ($18\%$ and $28\%$). Because $\dot u=g\dot u_0+u_0\dot g$, the second term can add discontinuities, so this point needs rate limiting or hysteresis before any online claim.

\subsection{Reselection Audit and Matched Controls}

Reselecting on validation data under the six clean constraints leaves $107$--$113$ of the $216$ candidates feasible and returns the same rate limits $(\lambda^{\downarrow},\lambda^{\uparrow})=(0.01,0.005)$ for all three seeds, with softness $0.5$, $1$, and $1$; validation fault-side $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ falls by $21.9\%$--$31.4\%$ while clean retention stays at $0.951$--$0.965$ and clean torque rate drops to $0.926$--$0.945$ times ungated. Rate limiting therefore removes the smoothness cost rather than trading retention for it: at the selected point fault-side torque rate is $0.184$ against $0.301$ unsmoothed and $0.251$ ungated, at matched $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ ($0.0375$ versus $0.0370$) and adequacy ($0.539$ versus $0.543$). Hysteresis is not usable: it saturates to full shutdown on every scenario including clean, so its $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ of zero is the trivial no-assistance solution.

With all six channels the gate reaches fault-side $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ $0.0456$ on ID and $0.0289$ on HT tasks against $0.0565$ and $0.0431$ ungated, at least as safe as every leave-one-out variant and as the Detector-gate subset ($0.0460$, $0.0305$). Only removing reconstruction degrades it measurably ($0.0471$, $0.0311$); removing the fault logit is bit-identical, and fault supervision or staleness alone reproduces the ungated command exactly, never firing. An oracle instantaneous-error comparator is \emph{worse} ($0.0527$, $0.0372$), so it is no upper bound under a fixed mapping. Matched randomization is the weak point. Against a random \emph{shutdown} control matched on full-shutdown fraction the gate wins all $60$ cells, but against random attenuation matched exactly on aligned-command retention ($1000$ draws, within $10^{-5}$) it is indistinguishable in $32$ of $60$ cells and worse on encoder dropout and a frozen IMU. With retention fixed, the benefit is attributable to attenuation magnitude, not selective timing.

\subsection{Transients, Paired Effects, and Reverse Ablation}

Sub-window corruptions defeat the rate-limited point almost completely: ID miss rates are $0.983$, $0.982$, $0.979$, $0.966$, and $0.910$ at $25$--$500$~ms, improving only to $0.822$ when the fault persists to the window end ($0.971$ to $0.902$, then $0.784$, on HT tasks). The rate limit is the cause. Among windows that do cross $g<0.5$ the onset delay tracks the injected duration itself ($1.1$ to $254.6$~ms on ID tasks), so a short transient ends before the gain can descend. The same limit keeps the point quiet---false shutdowns are $0.44$--$0.71$ per minute on ID and $1.53$--$1.98$ on HT faults, against clean pseudo-onset controls at $0.478$ and $1.566$, so residual alarms belong to the operating point and task shift, not the faults. Steady-state safety and transient responsiveness thus conflict under one rate limit.

Same-window paired double differences give a negative $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ estimate in $48$ of $54$ cells and an interval excluding zero in $49$, over $13{,}041$ matched pairs with none dropped, and $73$ of $90$ across-seed contrasts are sign-consistent. But two held-out participants make the participant-level bootstrap degenerate, so all $270$ cells are underpowered: pairing removes within-participant variance, not between-participant degrees of freedom.
The reverse ablation evaluates seven cells under one split, one $216$-candidate gate protocol, and one corruption realization against a single full-stack reference, removing the duplicate-reference and mixed-protocol confounds. Four cells lie on the $(X_{\mathrm{opp}}$, clean retention) front: no forecasting ($0.0440$ at $0.968$), the six-channel stack ($0.0456$ at $0.968$), no staleness ($0.0458$ at $0.972$), and the validation-selected subset ($0.0460$ at $0.986$). The three dominated cells order stably in every seed: no fault supervision ($0.0503$), no reconstruction ($0.0505$), and the deterministic variant ($0.0553$). The front is unresolvable: its values span $0.0020$ against a $0.0043$ gap to the nearest dominated cell, and the forecast-free cell ranks fourth, third, then first across seeds, so necessity holds only against the dominated cells.

\subsection{Ablations and Stress Tests}

Adding fault augmentation, probabilistic regression, reconstruction, and forecasting in a fixed non-factorial order under a fixed split and budget changes Detector-all AUROC by $+0.042\pm0.036$, $-0.083\pm0.003$, $+0.024\pm0.004$, and $-0.001\pm0.008$: the simpler stage-2 detector ranks highest, and reconstruction is the first stage whose selected score activates the gate, so these entries diagnose the sequence rather than identify an optimal architecture.
At maximum packet loss, Detector-all and Detector-gate reach $0.961$ ID and $0.914$ HT AUROC; at the longest delay they diverge to $0.899/0.743$ and $0.788/0.588$ because only the diagnostic coherence channel responds strongly. Across 15 leave-one-subject-out folds the non-increasing wrong-direction reduction averages $0.44$ percentage points (descriptive $95\%$ interval $[0.23,0.67]$), and clean retention averages $0.961$ with minimum $0.880$; the supplement reports fold-level results.

\section{Discussion}
Reliability estimates matter only if they change the command, not merely report confidence. The validation-selected gate yields a clean-adjusted $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ reduction of $0.0070$ $(\mathrm{Nm/kg})^2\mathrm{s}$ while keeping $96.1\%$ of the clean aligned command: a modest offline effect at one operating point.
The wrong-direction count captures only exact shutdowns, and the torque-rate increase exposes a trade-off AUROC or retention alone would hide; constrained reselection resolves it, returning one rate-limited point across seeds that is smoother than ungated at equal $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ and adequacy. The matched controls are more sobering: with retention fixed to within $10^{-5}$, random attenuation matches the six-channel gate in over half the cells, so the effect is one of attenuation magnitude rather than selective timing, and an oracle error comparator performing worse confirms that instantaneous error is not what a fixed mapping should chase.

Fault mechanisms violate different signal properties---reconstruction for a missing insole on ID tasks, staleness for packet loss and the insole under task shift, coherence for partial channel loss and delay---yet only removing reconstruction changes $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ materially, so mechanism diversity in the \emph{detector} has not yet transferred to the \emph{actuator}. With clean RMSE also worse than the deterministic TCN, predictive uncertainty and auxiliary training are not universal solutions. The layer complements approaches that expand behavioral coverage \citep{molinaro2024task} or reduce data demands \citep{scherpereel2025domain}, but score references and the operating point need re-estimation per sensor domain.

\section{Limitations}
The score responds to prediction error in general, and the cohort is the ceiling: pairing supplies the window-level contrast, but two held-out participants make the participant-level bootstrap degenerate, so every paired interval describes this cohort's dispersion, not a population effect. The LOSO bootstrap likewise treats held-out participants as resampling units despite overlapping training sets. The gate policy and audit protocol were designed after inspecting archived test diagnostics; although the selector uses validation data only, reevaluating the same test participants is post-hoc, not confirmation on a fresh cohort.
The matched controls do not support a timing claim. Under exact retention matching, random attenuation is indistinguishable from the six-channel gate in $32$ of $60$ cells and safer on two fault types, and two channels never fire, so the gain is one of magnitude and of the reconstruction channel rather than of fusion or of when the gate acts. Reported Detector-gate AUROC uses a selected subset while the actuation gate fuses all channels, the ordered ablation is non-factorial, and the stage-2 detector still has higher AUROC.
Sub-window transients bound the result harder: at the selected point $91$--$98\%$ of 25--500~ms ID faults and $90$--$97\%$ on HT tasks are missed, and even persistent faults are missed in $78$--$82\%$ of windows, so steady-state safety and transient responsiveness cannot both come from one rate limit. False shutdowns persist in the clean pseudo-onset controls, rising from $0.478$ to $1.566$ per minute under task shift. Only two fault pairs were tested, the three held-out operators stay within known packet-loss and delay families, and synthetic replay cannot represent hardware failures.
We did not run the gate online; fixed-kinematics replay supports relative command comparisons, not interaction safety, adaptation, or metabolic benefit, and the offline finding that rate limiting removes the torque-rate increase at no retention cost is unvalidated on hardware. Labels and sensing come from one platform, we do not jointly train the domain adaptation of \citet{scherpereel2025domain}, and low-level torque limits remain necessary.

\section{Conclusion}
We connected heterogeneous anomaly evidence to offline command consequences and ran the controls such a claim requires. Under one split-locked six-channel point per seed, gating attenuates opposing command at modest clean cost and rate limiting makes it smoother than ungated for free, but retention-matched randomization shows a magnitude effect rather than selective timing, and sub-window faults are mostly missed. This supports a reproducible offline audit, not an optimal architecture or a safety claim.

\section{Ethical Statement}
Reliability-aware assistance may reduce risk from silent sensing failures, but overconfident claims could encourage premature deployment, so we separate offline replay from human-subject evidence and make no safety guarantee. Data is used under source terms.
