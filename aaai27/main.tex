\begin{abstract}
Task-agnostic exoskeleton controllers map wearable-sensor histories directly to biological joint moments, so sensor faults can propagate into assistance even when nominal prediction error is low.
We study deployment-time reliability under participant, task, and synthetic sensor-fault shifts: anomaly evidence should alter the command without unnecessarily suppressing valid assistance.
Our framework combines probabilistic moment estimation, learned consistency signals, and mechanism-specific checks, then maps causal, validation-normalized evidence to a nonnegative torque gain.
Participant-disjoint offline replay covers in-distribution and training-held-out tasks, six training-time faults, and three held-out operators within the packet-loss and delay families.
Relative to a split-matched deterministic TCN, reliability training increases clean RMSE by $0.011$ Nm/kg in-distribution and $0.021$ Nm/kg on training-held-out tasks.
Across nine faults and both task splits, gating reduces the opposition-weighted torque-product integral by $0.0114$ $(\mathrm{Nm/kg})^2\mathrm{s}$, or $0.0070\pm0.0015$ after subtracting the matched clean-condition effect, while retaining $96.1\%$ of the ungated estimator's clean aligned command.
The mechanically non-increasing wrong-direction fraction falls by $0.74$ percentage points only at exact shutdowns; across fifteen leave-one-subject-out folds its reduction averages $0.44$ percentage points (descriptive $95\%$ bootstrap interval $[0.23,0.67]$).
Mean absolute torque rate nevertheless rises by $18\%$--$28\%$ on training-held-out faulty trials.
An audit excludes a configuration-mismatched reselection pass; post-hoc timing controls and sub-window transients around the archived operating point remain weak.
The evidence therefore supports a modest offline attenuation effect, not an online safety claim, and motivates reporting discrimination, magnitude-sensitive consequences, retention, smoothness, and matched controls together.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Powered lower-limb exoskeletons can reduce locomotor effort and support people with gait impairments, but controllers must decide what assistance is appropriate as the user moves among activities \citep{young2017state,kim2019exosuit,awad2017soft,siviy2023opportunities}.
Mode-based controllers decide from a fixed task set \citep{camargo2021classification,kang2022classification}, which scales poorly to transitions and unstructured motion; task-agnostic control instead estimates biological hip and knee moments from wearable sensors and uses this continuous physiological state to assist cyclic, transient, and unstructured movements \citep{molinaro2022hip,molinaro2024unifies,molinaro2024task}.

Wider task coverage, however, does not ensure that every deployed prediction is reliable.
Frozen streams, drift, burst packet loss, or asynchronous modalities can corrupt an otherwise familiar trial, and because predicted moments feed the torque pipeline, such errors may remove useful assistance or reverse its direction.
Clean RMSE cannot reveal these isolated events, predictive uncertainty alone need not detect a held but locally plausible value or slow drift \citep{ovadia2019trust}, and detecting an anomaly still does not specify what the actuator should do.
The question is therefore whether such an estimator can detect several forms of sensor failure and respond without unnecessarily suppressing valid assistance.

We address it with a causal multi-head TCN trained on synthetic faults, predicting joint moments, data-dependent uncertainty, fault probability, clean-input reconstruction, and short-horizon sensor values. A separate monitor combines these outputs with causal integrity checks, selects evaluation subsets without test data, and maps normalized timestep evidence to a continuous torque gate. We contribute a split-explicit fault-transfer protocol, a validation-frozen causal gate evaluated with magnitude-sensitive control metrics, and an audit suite of matched controls, transients, paired contrasts, and component ablations.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Task-agnostic exoskeleton control.}
Conventional controllers infer a discrete locomotion mode or gait phase before applying a task-specific torque policy \citep{camargo2021classification,kang2022classification}.
Moment-based control instead uses an instantaneous biological joint-moment estimate as a continuous control state, first for subject-independent hip assistance \citep{shepherd2022deep,molinaro2022hip}, then as a representation shared across ambulatory conditions \citep{molinaro2024unifies}, and finally for coordinated hip and knee assistance without task classification \citep{molinaro2024task}, whose sensing configuration, prediction target, and control transformation we adopt. Those studies evaluate accuracy under nominal operation; their sensor ablations measure sensitivity to removing a modality but do not estimate sensor health, calibrate deployment risk, or change assistance when a fault is detected.

\paragraph{Data efficiency and domain shift.}
Because moment labels need motion-capture and force-plate measurement and are device-specific, \citet{scherpereel2025domain} translate open-source biomechanics data into a target sensor domain by CycleGAN; personalization \citep{zhang2017humanloop,slade2022personalizing} and simulation-trained policies \citep{luo2024simulation} likewise act before deployment. All change how the estimator or its assistance profile is obtained; we address corrupted observations encountered afterwards on the target device.

\paragraph{Uncertainty, anomalies, and selective prediction.}
Heteroscedastic regression separates input-dependent observation noise from the predicted mean \citep{kendall2017uncertainties}, while MC dropout and deep ensembles approximate epistemic uncertainty \citep{gal2016dropout,lakshminarayanan2017ensembles}, though both may become miscalibrated under shift \citep{ovadia2019trust}.
Uncertainty-aware ankle assistance disengages on OOD estimates, targeting behavioral novelty rather than structured sensor faults \citep{tourk2025uncertainty}. In multivariate time series, reconstruction error and changes in inter-variable relationships supply evidence that predictive variance misses \citep{song2023memto,dai2024sarad}, motivating mechanism-specific channels.
Selective prediction abstains when expected error is high \citep{geifman2019selectivenet}; an assistive controller cannot abstain without saying what the actuator should do, so we convert validation-normalized anomaly evidence into a continuous gain and evaluate the resulting command trade-offs.

\paragraph{Fault-tolerant exoskeleton control.}
Prior exoskeleton studies inject freeze, stuck-at-zero, and bit-flip faults into dynamic models to identify critical exposure durations \citep{fabarisov2020fault}, or use model-based observers to reconstruct sensor and actuator faults before trajectory tracking \citep{mokhtari2022fault,sun2025reconstruction}, exploiting an explicit plant model for rehabilitation trajectory control.
We instead monitor a learned, task-agnostic moment estimator from heterogeneous wearable observations; it detects but does not reconstruct faults, so its offline replay complements rather than replaces plant-level fault isolation and certified hardware limits.

\section{Problem Formulation}

Let $\mathbf{x}_{1:T}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times T}$ denote a causal input window of unilateral measurements from the foot, shank, and thigh IMUs, the insole, and the hip and knee encoders, and let the target $\mathbf{y}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$ hold mass-normalized biological hip and knee moments.
During deployment, an unknown corruption $f\in\mathcal{F}$ may transform the observations and produce $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1:T}$.
We seek a predictor $\widehat{\mathbf{y}}_t$ and a reliability gate $g_t\in[0,1]$ such that the commanded torque
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\tau}^{\mathrm{cmd}}_t
    = g_t\,\mathcal{C}(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}_{1:t})
\end{equation}
preserves useful assistance for clean inputs while suppressing harmful commands under task shifts or sensor faults, where $\mathcal{C}$ is the baseline pipeline for moment-to-torque scaling, delay, filtering, and saturation.
Evaluation must cover moment fidelity, fault discrimination, command attenuation, retained baseline assistance, and smoothness; none of these criteria suffices alone.

\subsection{Reliability-Aware Causal TCN}

Training-participant channel means $\mathbf{c}$ and standard deviations $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ normalize each observation as $\mathbf{z}_t=(\mathbf{x}_t-\mathbf{c})\oslash\max(\mathbf{v},10^{-6})$, with $\oslash$ elementwise division.
Following \citet{molinaro2024task} and \citet{bai2018tcn}, the shared encoder has $L=5$ residual blocks, each with two causal convolutions of kernel size $k=5$ and dilation $2^{\ell-1}$, weight normalization, ReLU, and dropout.
The receptive field is $H=1+2(k-1)\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}2^{\ell-1}=249$ samples, and the first $H-1$ window outputs are excluded from training and evaluation.

Five $1\!\times\!1$ heads produce the moment mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$, log variance $\mathbf{s}_t$ (clamped to $[-8,5]$), binary fault logit $a_t$, clean-input reconstruction $\widehat{\mathbf{z}}_t$, and $h$-step forecast $\overline{\mathbf{z}}_{t+h\mid t}$, giving the diagonal predictive distribution $p(\mathbf{y}_t\mid\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1:t})=\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t,\operatorname{diag}(\exp\mathbf{s}_t))$.

\subsection{Fault-Augmented Multi-Task Learning}

For each training window, we sample clean data or one of six faults through an operator $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\mathcal{A}_f(\mathbf{x};\boldsymbol{\xi})$ at fixed severity and target group: the insole or encoder group is zeroed; the shank IMU takes a bias ramp of $0.25$ channel standard deviations; packet loss holds each sample independently with probability $0.15$ across all groups; the foot IMU freezes; or sensor delay shifts every group by $10$ samples ($50$~ms), scaled by $1.0$, $1.6$, $0.6$, $2.0$, and $1.2$ for foot, shank, thigh, insole, and encoder, so modalities become mutually asynchronous rather than uniformly late.
Each fault occupies its whole window, so the fault label $b_f$ below is window-constant.
The three held-out operators reuse known fault families at the same nominal rates: burst loss forward-fills runs of length $\max(2,\operatorname{round}(2+24p))$ with per-timestep start probability $p$ divided by that length, partial loss draws an independent channel--time mask, and delay jitter redraws each modality's shift per window in $[0.5,1.5]$ times nominal.
They test operator transfer within known packet-loss and delay semantics, not open-set hardware-fault generalization; the supplement lists every protocol role.

With $m_{jt}$ indicating a valid moment label and $b_f=\mathbb{I}[f\neq\mathrm{clean}]$, the masked heteroscedastic loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mom}}
=\frac{\sum_{j,t}m_{jt}\left[s_{jt}+(y_{jt}-\mu_{jt})^2\exp(-s_{jt})\right]}{2\sum_{j,t}m_{jt}}.
\end{equation}
The moment head receives $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}$ with the clean target $\mathbf{y}$, encouraging robustness when a fault leaves enough information, and the fault head uses timestepwise binary cross-entropy $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fault}}$ between $b_f$ and $\sigma(a_t)$, averaged over the set $\Omega$ of timesteps with at least one valid joint label.
The auxiliary heads recover the uncorrupted normalized sequence instead of reproducing the corrupted input, as $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}=\operatorname{MSE}_{\Omega}(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}_t(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}),\mathbf{z}_t(\mathbf{x}))$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}=\operatorname{MSE}_{\Omega}(\overline{\mathbf{z}}_{t+h\mid t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1:t}),\mathbf{z}_{t+h}(\mathbf{x}))$.
The complete objective is $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mom}}+\lambda_f\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fault}}+\lambda_r\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}+\lambda_p\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}$ with $(\lambda_f,\lambda_r,\lambda_p)=(0.2,0.1,0.1)$.
Reconstruction updates the shared encoder, whereas forecasting stops gradients at $\mathbf{h}_t$ so that it acts as a temporal probe.

\subsection{Complementary Integrity Signals}

Different faults affect different signal properties, so the monitor considers eight candidates. The learned ones are fault probability $q_t^{\mathrm{fault}}=\sigma(a_t)$, mean aleatoric scale $q_t^{\mathrm{ale}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_j\exp(s_{jt}/2)$, reconstruction and forecast residuals, and optional epistemic scale.
Over modality groups $G$, the residual scores are $q_t^{\mathrm{rec}}=\max_G|G|^{-1}\sum_{c\in G}(\widehat z_{ct}-\widetilde z_{ct})^2$ and $q_{t+h}^{\mathrm{pred}}=\max_G|G|^{-1}\sum_{c\in G}(\overline z_{c,t+h\mid t}-\widetilde z_{c,t+h})^2$.
A forecast made at $t$ is scored only when its target arrives at $t+h$, so the score at $u$ compares $\overline{\mathbf{z}}_{u\mid u-h}$ with $\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}_u$ and the channel is zero for the first $h$ samples, preserving command-time causality. Epistemic scale is the joint-averaged standard deviation across stochastic-dropout passes or ensemble members.

Three integrity statistics cover signatures that learned heads may miss. Staleness is the moving fraction of nearly unchanged channels and coherence is one minus the mean absolute rolling correlation between differenced, corresponding IMU axes; both use trailing windows, so their value at $t$ needs no future sample. Drift is the largest modality-averaged channel slope, fitted over the whole window and therefore the only noncausal, window-level channel.
The evaluated gate consumes staleness but not coherence or drift, so both are diagnostic channels: for drift this is forced by its definition, for coherence it is an implementation choice whose cost the severity analysis quantifies. Of the two, only coherence is ever selected.

\subsection{Validation-Normalized Fault Evidence}

For validation window $i$, each channel is averaged over its valid timesteps $\Omega_i$ to obtain $Q_{ik}=|\Omega_i|^{-1}\sum_{t\in\Omega_i}q_{it}^{k}$. The detector reference is $\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}}=\operatorname{Quantile}_{0.9}(\{Q_{ik}:i\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{clean}}\})$ over clean validation windows, and a subset $S$ fuses as $R_i^{\mathrm{det}}(S)=\max_{k\in S}Q_{ik}/\max(\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}},10^{-6})$.
Using five validation faults (insole missing, encoder dropout, IMU bias, packet loss, and sensor delay), we enumerate subsets of at most three channels and, given their validation-fault AUROCs $A_f(S)$, maximize
\begin{equation}
    J_{\mathrm{det}}(S)=\operatorname{mean}_f A_f(S)
    \!+\!0.25\min_f A_f(S)\!-\!0.002(|S|\!-\!1),
\end{equation}
where the second term rewards the hardest fault and the last favors smaller subsets.
Both $S$ and $\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}}$ are frozen before test evaluation, and burst loss, partial-channel loss, and delay jitter are excluded from training and detector selection alike.

Coherence and drift sit outside the evaluated gate, so one fused number would overstate what is available to deployment.
We run the same subset search twice on the same validation scores: \emph{Detector-all} searches all eight candidates as an offline diagnostic reference, while \emph{Detector-gate} restricts the pool to the channels actually implemented in $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}$ below.
These subsets are used only for window-level AUROC evaluation.
The gate does not reuse the selected detector subset: it fuses all available members of $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}$ and tunes the actuation mapping separately.

\subsection{Validation-Selected Assistance Gate}

The command-time anomaly score uses the causal timestep-level channels
$\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}=\{\mathrm{fault},\mathrm{ale},\mathrm{rec},\mathrm{pred},\mathrm{epi},\mathrm{stale}\}$ that are available in a given configuration, fused as $r_t=\max_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}}q_t^k/\max(\rho_k^{\mathrm{gate}},10^{-6})$.
We share the detector scale, $\rho_k^{\mathrm{gate}}:=\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}}$, to place window- and timestep-level scores on the same numerical scale; it is a quantile of window means, not a timestep quantile or a calibrated posterior probability. Clean training-held-out-task validation trials and designated validation faults select the deadband $d$ and softness $\gamma$.

The score is converted into a raw continuous gain $\widetilde g_t=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}(1-[r_t-(1+d)]/\gamma)$, so $\widetilde g_t=1$ within the nominal region $r_t\leq1+d$ and decreases linearly beyond it.
The deadband is $d=\min(2,\max[0.1,\operatorname{Quantile}_{0.9}(r^{\mathrm{val\text{-}held}})-1])$ on clean training-held-out-task trials from validation participants, and we search $\gamma\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2,4,8\}$.
The archived evaluation required at least 95\% clean aligned-command retention and recorded non-increase checks on the clean wrong-direction fraction and four validation faults (insole missing, encoder dropout, packet loss, and sensor delay); candidates were ranked by $\kappa_{\mathrm{clean}}+2\bar\delta+0.25\bar\kappa$, with $\kappa_{\mathrm{clean}}$ the gated-over-ungated clean retention and $\bar\delta,\bar\kappa$ the mean relative wrong-direction reduction and retention ratio over validation faults.
This exactly describes the archived operating point, but its non-increase checks are not substantive constraints: because $g_t\geq0$, the gate cannot create a sign reversal, so the only active feasibility condition is clean retention and $\bar\delta$ merely ranks hard closures.
We therefore reselect the operating point on validation data using $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ as the objective under six explicit clean-side constraints---aligned-command and capped-overlap retention, tracking RMSE, full-shutdown fraction, mean absolute torque rate, and false shutdowns per minute---searching $\gamma$ jointly with the causal rate limits $(\lambda^{\downarrow},\lambda^{\uparrow})$ over $216$ candidates per seed.
Unlike wrong-direction non-increase, none of these six constraints is guaranteed by nonnegative gating.
The rate limiter is causal: starting from $\widetilde g_t$, it applies the lower bound $g_{t-1}-\lambda^{\downarrow}$ and upper bound $g_{t-1}+\lambda^{\uparrow}$ only when the corresponding limit is positive; a grid value of zero leaves that direction unrestricted. It therefore neither anticipates onsets nor changes what the gate is allowed to see.
The baseline transformation $\mathcal{C}$ scales the estimated hip and knee moments by $0.20$ and $0.15$, applies the configured joint delays and a 6-Hz low-pass filter, and clips torque to $0.45$ Nm/kg; the gated command $\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\mathrm{cmd}}_t=g_t\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1:t})$ attenuates unreliable estimates but does not replace independent actuator saturation or hardware limits.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Data, Splits, and Comparisons}

We use the released exoskeleton sensor and biological-moment data underlying the task-agnostic controller \citep{molinaro2024task,scherpereel2023dataset}, resampled to a uniform $200$~Hz, so one sample is $5$~ms and the forecast horizon $h=1$ is one control step.
Inputs comprise $25$ unilateral channels in five modality groups $G$: foot, shank, and thigh IMU ($6$ channels each, three gyroscope and three accelerometer axes), insole ($3$), and hip--knee encoders ($4$), with the action profiles of the input ablation adding a sixth group; targets are right hip and knee moments in Nm/kg.
Of the $15$ participants, $11$ train, $2$ validate, and $2$ test. Jumping, cutting, weight lifting, and lunges are excluded from estimator fitting and form the training-held-out task set, written HT below against ID for in-distribution tasks; clean trials from these categories tune the gate on validation participants only, so they are not unseen to the complete pipeline. No test participant informs preprocessing, score normalization, detector selection, or gate tuning. The leave-one-subject-out analysis rotates over all $15$, each fold taking the preceding participant as validation; the primary baseline is a deterministic, non-augmented TCN on the same split, and ablations add fault augmentation, probabilistic regression, reconstruction, and forecasting in order.

\subsection{Metrics and Implementation}

We report RMSE, MAE, $R^2$, Gaussian NLL, uncertainty--error correlation, interval coverage and calibration error, and per-channel and fused fault AUROC. Control-replay metrics are defined against the ideal command $\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\star}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{y})$, the \emph{true} moment through the same transformation $\mathcal{C}$.
A joint-timestep pair $n$ is \emph{active} when both joint labels at its timestep are valid and $|\tau^{\star}_n|>0.02$ Nm/kg; the set $\mathcal{A}$ depends only on the true moment, so it is identical for gated and ungated commands and cannot be shrunk by attenuation.
Writing $u_n=\tau^{\mathrm{cmd}}_n$, our primary command-consequence metric is the opposition-weighted torque-product integral
\begin{equation}
X_{\mathrm{opp}}(u)=\textstyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}}
\max(0,-u_n\tau^{\star}_n)\,\Delta t,\quad \Delta t=1/200\ {\rm s},
\end{equation}
in $(\mathrm{Nm/kg})^2\mathrm{s}$; because no angular velocity appears it is an exposure surrogate, not mechanical energy.
We additionally report the wrong-direction fraction
$w(u)=|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{I}[u_n\tau_n^\star<0]$, peak $|u_n|$ over those pairs, mean absolute torque rate $|\Delta u|/\Delta t$ in Nm\,kg$^{-1}$\,s$^{-1}$, and command retention $\kappa=\sum_{\mathcal{A}}\max(0,u_n^{\mathrm{gated}}\operatorname{sgn}\tau_n^\star)/\sum_{\mathcal{A}}\max(0,u_n^{\mathrm{ungated}}\operatorname{sgn}\tau_n^\star)$, a weighted fraction of the baseline's same-direction command rather than a tracking measure.
For the clean-side constraint, capped-overlap adequacy replaces each same-direction magnitude by $\min(|u_n|,|\tau_n^\star|)$ and normalizes by $\sum_{\mathcal A}|\tau_n^\star|$; capped-overlap retention is its gated-over-ungated ratio, so over-assistance is not rewarded.
Since $u_n^{\mathrm{gated}}=g_nu_n^{\mathrm{ungated}}$ with $g_n\geq0$, the wrong-direction indicator factorizes into $\mathbb{I}[u_n^{\mathrm{ungated}}\tau_n^\star<0]\,\mathbb{I}[g_n>0]$, so $w(u^{\mathrm{gated}})\leq w(u^{\mathrm{ungated}})$ and $w$ responds only to exact shutdowns ($g_n=0$). Likewise, $X_{\mathrm{opp}}(gu)\leq X_{\mathrm{opp}}(u)$, but it responds continuously to every attenuation $0\leq g_n<1$; retention and matched controls are therefore necessary to distinguish selective timing from generic suppression.
A first-order actuator proxy also estimates net-moment RMS and work-related quantities, as relative offline comparisons rather than metabolic outcomes.
The model has five 80-channel TCN blocks, kernel size 5, dropout 0.15, and 768-sample windows at stride 384 with the first 248 outputs masked, trained for 20 epochs with AdamW ($10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) under seeds 7, 13, and 23; Gaussian NLL omits the additive $\frac12\log(2\pi)$ constant. We report mean, standard deviation, and paired seed-level differences.
Three seeds quantify only training stochasticity, not participant uncertainty. For descriptive subject transfer, we summarize the fifteen leave-one-subject-out fold effects with a percentile-bootstrap $95\%$ interval over folds ($10{,}000$ resamples, fixed seed) on each subject's reduction macro-averaged over nine faults and both task splits; overlapping training sets and the non-increase identity preclude a population-level interpretation.
Over ten scenarios, two splits, and several metrics an uncorrected test family would be uninterpretable and a corrected one underpowered at three seeds, so per-scenario entries and this fold interval are descriptive rather than confirmatory.

\subsection{Fault Detection under Task Shift}

Figure~\ref{fig:detection-taxonomy} reports all nine operators and all eight candidate channels. Across the six training-time faults, Detector-gate reaches macro-AUROC $0.815\pm0.006$ on ID and $0.740\pm0.002$ on HT tasks, against $0.849\pm0.005$ and $0.773\pm0.002$ for Detector-all; for the three held-out operators the pairs are $0.812\pm0.011$ and $0.715\pm0.002$ against $0.842\pm0.010$ and $0.778\pm0.004$.
The gap is concentrated rather than uniform: validation selects $\{$reconstruction residual, staleness, coherence$\}$ for Detector-all and $\{$reconstruction residual, staleness$\}$ for Detector-gate, and losing coherence costs fixed sensor delay $0.878\to0.628$ on ID and $0.774\to0.557$ on HT tasks, with delay jitter behaving similarly ($0.867\to0.641$, $0.766\to0.559$).
Coherence also reverses direction for held-out burst loss (AUROC $0.055$ ID, $0.054$ HT): its clean scores then sit above its faulty ones and max fusion inherits that raised baseline, so dropping it improves ID burst loss from $0.743$ to $0.886$. This exposes the high-score-is-anomalous assumption of max fusion rather than supporting an open-set claim.
Averaged over all nine faults, staleness is the strongest single channel ($0.785$ ID, $0.796$ HT); coherence is the most extreme rather than the broadest, reaching $1.000$ for partial channel loss and a stuck IMU but $0.658$ and $0.663$ on average, while IMU bias stays the hardest fused case at $0.502$ and $0.504$, near chance and so barely distinguishable from valid motion with these sensors.

Figure~\ref{fig:safety-utility} makes $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$, rather than the hard-shutdown-sensitive count $w$, the primary consequence view.
On HT tasks $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ falls from $0.0439\pm0.0062$ to $0.0298\pm0.0030$ $(\mathrm{Nm/kg})^2\mathrm{s}$ under training-time faults and from $0.0412\pm0.0066$ to $0.0270\pm0.0041$ for held-out operators, with peak wrong-direction torque dropping from $0.175\pm0.025$ to $0.137\pm0.017$ Nm/kg.
The effect is smaller on ID faults ($0.0581\to0.0469$), negligible on clean ID trials ($0.0408\to0.0400$), and substantial on clean HT trials ($0.0316\to0.0236$): the gate also attenuates ordinary task-shift error.
Subtracting the clean gate change within each seed and split before macro-averaging the nine matched scenarios, the fault-side change of $-0.0114\pm0.0020$ against a matched clean change of $-0.0044\pm0.0009$ leaves a clean-adjusted contrast of $-0.0070\pm0.0015$; for $w$ the three values are $-0.736\pm0.067$, $-0.397\pm0.043$, and $-0.339\pm0.098$ percentage points, so only about $46\%$ of the count reduction lies beyond the clean-condition effect.

The attenuation has two costs. Clean aligned-command retention is $0.960$ ID and $0.961$ on HT tasks but falls to $0.829$--$0.948$ under faults, and mean absolute torque rate rises from $0.237$ to $0.279$ Nm\,kg$^{-1}$\,s$^{-1}$ on HT seen faults and $0.249$ to $0.318$ for held-out operators ($18\%$ and $28\%$). Because $\dot u=g\dot u_0+u_0\dot g$, the second term can add discontinuities, so the archived point cannot support an online claim without rate limiting or hysteresis.

\subsection{Reselection Audit and Matched Controls}

The constrained rule searches $216$ validation candidates per seed, but its first execution used \texttt{stairs,ramp} as held-out tasks whereas the primary experiment uses \texttt{jump,cutting,lift\_weight,lunges}. Because this changes both ID and HT membership, that pass is excluded from comparable evidence; the corrected driver now reads split and window settings from each archived main report and rejects drift before evaluating test data.
In a separate exploratory sweep around the archived Detector-gate subset, $36$ rate-limited settings beat the ungated command on fault-side $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ at no torque-rate cost, the best reaching $0.0278$ versus $0.0620$ with torque rate $0.096$ versus $0.267$ but only $0.50$ clean aligned-command adequacy; hysteresis reaches $0.0083$ at $0.42$. Safety and smoothness are jointly attainable in that diagnostic sweep; surviving nominal command pays.

The same exploratory subset is only weakly separated from randomized timing. Permuting its gate values is indistinguishable in $11$ of $120$ cells ($1000$ draws each), including cells where the permutation is marginally safer; this preserves the gate-value distribution but does not exactly match the aligned-command retention statistic. The strongest cell against a random \emph{shutdown} control reaches percentile $0.272$. Among leave-one-out six-channel gates only removing reconstruction measurably degrades fault-side $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ ($0.0493$ versus $0.0479$); removing the fault logit is bit-identical, and fault supervision or staleness alone exactly reproduces the ungated command ($0.0565$), never firing here. An oracle error comparator reaches $0.0534$, \emph{worse} than the selected-subset gate, so oracle instantaneous error is no upper bound under a fixed mapping.

\subsection{Transients, Paired Effects, and Reverse Ablation}

Confining corruptions to sub-windows degrades the archived selected-subset gate sharply with duration. On ID tasks the miss rate is $0.945$, $0.933$, $0.898$, $0.814$, and $0.767$ at $25$, $50$, $100$, $250$, and $500$~ms and reaches $0.551$ for faults persisting to the window end; the corresponding HT range is $0.921$ to $0.769$, reaching $0.553$ when persistent. Among windows that cross $g<0.5$, onset delays span $5.2$--$612$~ms on ID and $3.1$--$583$~ms on HT tasks. False shutdowns are $18.9$ per minute on ID and $46$--$47$ on HT conditions, matching clean pseudo-onset controls at $18.9$ and $46.2$, so the false-alarm rate belongs to the operating point and task shift, not the injected faults.

For the same exploratory subset, same-window paired double differences give a negative $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ point estimate in $52$ of $54$ cells over $13{,}041$ exactly matched pairs, none dropped, but two held-out participants make the participant-level bootstrap degenerate, so all $216$ cells are underpowered: pairing removes within-participant variance, not between-participant degrees of freedom.
Five of eight reverse-ablation cells lie on the reported $(X_{\mathrm{opp}}$, clean aligned-command adequacy) front. However, two nominally full-stack rows were evaluated under different gate definitions or corruption draws and differ slightly ($0.0486$ versus $0.0487$), so the current table cannot establish that the full stack is dominated. Among trained variants, dropping forecasting has the largest $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ value ($0.0810$), the deterministic variant the lowest clean adequacy ($0.708$), and removing reconstruction is mildest; these comparisons remain descriptive until every cell is reevaluated on one frozen corruption realization and gate policy.

\subsection{Ablations and Stress Tests}

Adding fault augmentation, probabilistic regression, reconstruction, and forecasting in a fixed non-factorial order under a fixed split and budget changes Detector-all AUROC by $+0.042\pm0.036$, $-0.083\pm0.003$, $+0.024\pm0.004$, and $-0.001\pm0.008$: the simpler stage-2 detector ranks highest, and reconstruction is only the first stage whose selected score activates the gate, so these entries diagnose the sequence rather than identify a Pareto-optimal architecture.
At maximum packet loss, Detector-all and Detector-gate reach $0.961$ ID and $0.914$ HT AUROC; at the longest delay they diverge to $0.899/0.743$ and $0.788/0.588$ because only the diagnostic coherence channel responds strongly. Across 15 leave-one-subject-out folds the mechanically non-increasing wrong-direction reduction averages $0.44$ percentage points (descriptive bootstrap $95\%$ interval $[0.23,0.67]$), and clean retention averages $0.961$ with minimum $0.880$; the supplement reports full stress, ablation, and fold-level results.

\section{Discussion}
Reliability estimates matter only if they change the command, not merely report confidence. The validation-selected gate yields a clean-adjusted $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ reduction of $0.0070$ $(\mathrm{Nm/kg})^2\mathrm{s}$ while retaining $96.1\%$ of the clean baseline's aligned command: a modest offline effect at one operating point.
The wrong-direction count captures only exact shutdowns, and the torque-rate increase exposes a trade-off that AUROC or retention alone would hide; a correctly split reselection is required to determine whether non-vacuous constraints convert it into an acceptable adequacy cost. The exploratory timing controls are cautionary---gate-value permutation sometimes matches the selected-subset gate and an oracle error comparator does worse---but they do not yet isolate the timing value of the declared six-channel, reselected gate.

Fault mechanisms violate different signal properties---reconstruction for a missing insole on ID tasks, staleness for packet loss and the insole under task shift, coherence for partial channel loss and delay---yet in the exploratory gate comparison only removing reconstruction changes $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ materially. Mechanism diversity in the \emph{detector} therefore has not yet transferred convincingly to the \emph{actuator}. Clean RMSE is also worse than the split-matched deterministic TCN, so predictive uncertainty and auxiliary training are not universal solutions. The layer complements approaches that expand behavioral coverage \citep{molinaro2024task} or reduce training-data demands \citep{scherpereel2025domain}, but score references and the operating point must be re-estimated in each target sensor domain.

\section{Limitations}
The score responds to prediction error in general, and the cohort is the ceiling: pairing supplies the window-level contrast, but two held-out participants make the participant-level bootstrap degenerate, so every paired interval describes this cohort's dispersion, not a population effect. The LOSO bootstrap likewise treats held-out participants as resampling units despite overlapping training sets.
The corrected gate policy and audit protocol were designed after inspecting archived test diagnostics. Although the implemented selector itself uses validation data only, reevaluating the same test participants is post-hoc and is not equivalent to confirmation on a fresh cohort.
The matched controls do not yet support a fusion claim: they use the archived Detector-gate subset rather than the declared six-channel reselected gate, and their permutation preserves gate values rather than exactly matching weighted retention. Two command-time channels never fire in that audit and the oracle error comparator is worse, suggesting that attenuation magnitude and reconstruction dominate, but the final all-channel comparison must be rerun. Reported Detector-gate AUROC likewise uses a selected subset while the actuation gate fuses all available channels.
The first reselected point used the wrong held-task set and is not comparable to the primary split. The reverse-ablation table also mixes nominally duplicate full-stack evaluations with different corruption draws, so it cannot support a dominance claim; the ordered ablation is non-factorial and the stage-2 detector still has higher AUROC.
Sub-window transients bound detection further: $77$--$95\%$ of 25--500~ms ID faults and $77$--$92\%$ on HT tasks are missed by the archived selected-subset point; false shutdowns persist in both clean pseudo-onset controls and rise from $18.9$ to $46.2$ per minute under task shift. Only two simultaneous synthetic fault pairs were tested, the three held-out operators stay within known packet-loss and delay families, and synthetic replay cannot represent hardware failures.
We did not run the gate online; fixed-kinematics replay supports relative command comparisons, not interaction safety, adaptation, or metabolic benefit, and rate limiting removes the offline torque-rate increase only by trading retention, neither trade-off validated on hardware. Labels and sensing come from one platform, we do not jointly train the domain adaptation of \citet{scherpereel2025domain}, and independent low-level torque and hardware limits remain necessary.

\section{Conclusion}
We connected heterogeneous anomaly evidence to offline command consequences and implemented the controls such a claim requires: constrained reselection, randomized and oracle comparators, a rate-limit and hysteresis grid, sub-window transients, paired contrasts, and leave-one-component-out ablation. Their first pass exposes split, policy, and matching choices that must be unified before they support the declared six-channel gate, so we report a reproducible audit protocol rather than an optimal architecture or safety claim; split-consistent exact-retention all-channel reevaluation and online human-subject validation remain the next steps.

\section{Ethical Statement}
Reliability-aware assistance may reduce risk from silent sensing failures, but overconfident claims could encourage premature deployment around human users, so we separate offline replay from human-subject evidence, report negative and weak detector results, and do not present the gate as a safety guarantee. We use participant data under the source dataset's consent terms.
