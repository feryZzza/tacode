\begin{abstract}
Task-agnostic exoskeleton controllers map wearable-sensor histories directly to biological joint moments, allowing sensor faults to propagate into assistance even when nominal prediction error is low.
We study deployment-time reliability under participant, task, and synthetic sensor-fault shifts: anomaly evidence should change the command without unnecessarily suppressing the ungated estimator's valid assistance.
Our causal monitor combines probabilistic moment estimation, learned consistency signals, and mechanism-specific checks, then maps a validation-normalized anomaly score to a nonnegative torque gain.
Participant-disjoint evaluation covers in-distribution and training-held-out tasks, six training-time faults, and three held-out corruption operators within the packet-loss and delay families.
Relative to a split-matched deterministic TCN, reliability training increases clean-trial RMSE by $0.011$ Nm/kg in-distribution and $0.021$ Nm/kg on training-held-out tasks.
Macro-averaged over nine faults and both task splits, gating reduces the opposition-weighted torque-product integral by $0.0114$ $(\mathrm{Nm/kg})^2\mathrm{s}$; subtracting the matched clean-condition effect leaves $0.0070\pm0.0015$.
The wrong-direction fraction falls by $0.74$ percentage points, but this count changes only where the gain reaches zero; its clean-adjusted reduction is $0.34\pm0.10$ points.
Across clean trials, the gate retains $96.1\%$ of the ungated estimator's aligned command, while mean absolute torque rate rises by $18\%$--$28\%$ on training-held-out faulty trials.
Across fifteen leave-one-subject-out folds the per-subject reduction is $0.44$ percentage points ($95\%$ bootstrap CI $[0.23, 0.67]$) and positive for every participant.
The evidence supports a modest offline attenuation effect, not an online safety claim, and shows why discrimination, magnitude-sensitive control consequences, retention, and command smoothness must be reported together.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Powered lower-limb exoskeletons can reduce locomotor effort and support people with gait impairments, but controllers must decide what assistance is appropriate as the user moves among activities \citep{young2017state,kim2019exosuit,awad2017soft,siviy2023opportunities}.
Mode-based controllers decide from a fixed task set \citep{camargo2021classification,kang2022classification}, which scales poorly to transitions and unstructured motion; task-agnostic control instead estimates biological hip and knee moments from wearable sensors and uses this continuous physiological state to assist cyclic, transient, and unstructured movements \citep{molinaro2022hip,molinaro2024unifies,molinaro2024task}.

Wider task coverage, however, does not ensure that every deployed prediction is reliable.
Frozen streams, drift, burst packet loss, or asynchronous modalities can corrupt an otherwise familiar trial, and because predicted moments feed the torque pipeline, such errors may remove useful assistance or reverse its direction.
Clean RMSE cannot reveal these isolated events, predictive uncertainty alone need not detect a held but locally plausible value or slow drift \citep{ovadia2019trust}, and detecting an anomaly still does not specify what the actuator should do.
The question is therefore whether such an estimator can detect several forms of sensor failure and respond without unnecessarily suppressing valid assistance.

We address it with a causal multi-head TCN trained on synthetic faults, predicting joint moments, data-dependent uncertainty, fault probability, clean-input reconstruction, and short-horizon sensor values. A separate monitor combines these outputs with causal integrity checks, selects evaluation subsets without test data, and maps normalized timestep evidence to a continuous torque gate. Participant-disjoint splits separate task shift from held-out corruption operators, and we report detection together with magnitude-sensitive opposition exposure, wrong-direction counts, command retention, and torque rate.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Task-agnostic exoskeleton control.}
Conventional controllers infer a discrete locomotion mode or gait phase before applying a task-specific torque policy \citep{camargo2021classification,kang2022classification}.
Moment-based control instead uses an instantaneous biological joint-moment estimate as a continuous control state, first for subject-independent hip assistance \citep{shepherd2022deep,molinaro2022hip}, then as a common representation across ambulatory conditions \citep{molinaro2024unifies}, and finally for coordinated hip and knee assistance without task classification \citep{molinaro2024task}, whose sensing configuration, prediction target, and control transformation we adopt.
Those studies evaluate accuracy under nominal operation; their sensor ablations measure sensitivity to removing a modality but do not estimate sensor health, calibrate deployment risk, or change assistance when a fault is detected.

\paragraph{Data efficiency and domain shift.}
Moment labels require motion-capture and force-plate measurement and are device-specific, so \citet{scherpereel2025domain} translate open-source biomechanics data into a target sensor domain by CycleGAN, reducing the labeled target data needed.
Personalization \citep{zhang2017humanloop,slade2022personalizing} and simulation-trained policies \citep{luo2024simulation} likewise act before deployment.
All change how the estimator or its assistance profile is obtained; we address corrupted observations encountered afterwards on the target device.

\paragraph{Uncertainty, anomalies, and selective prediction.}
Heteroscedastic regression separates input-dependent observation noise from the predicted mean \citep{kendall2017uncertainties}, while MC dropout and deep ensembles approximate epistemic uncertainty \citep{gal2016dropout,lakshminarayanan2017ensembles}, though both may become miscalibrated under shift \citep{ovadia2019trust}.
Uncertainty-aware ankle assistance switches on OOD estimates, targeting behavioral novelty rather than structured sensor faults \citep{tourk2025uncertainty}.
For multivariate time series, reconstruction error and changes in inter-variable relationships give evidence that predictive variance misses \citep{song2023memto,dai2024sarad}, motivating mechanism-specific channels.
Selective prediction abstains when expected error is high \citep{geifman2019selectivenet}; an assistive controller cannot abstain without saying what the actuator should do, so we convert validation-normalized anomaly evidence into a continuous gain and evaluate the resulting command trade-offs.

\paragraph{Fault-tolerant exoskeleton control.}
Prior exoskeleton studies inject freeze, stuck-at-zero, and bit-flip faults into dynamic models to identify critical exposure durations \citep{fabarisov2020fault}, or use model-based observers to reconstruct sensor and actuator faults before trajectory tracking \citep{mokhtari2022fault,sun2025reconstruction}.
These approaches exploit an explicit plant model and target rehabilitation trajectory control.
Our setting instead monitors a learned, task-agnostic biological-moment estimator from heterogeneous wearable observations; it detects but does not reconstruct faults, and its offline replay therefore complements rather than replaces plant-level fault isolation and certified hardware limits.

\section{Problem Formulation}

Let $\mathbf{x}_{1:T}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times T}$ denote a causal input window of unilateral measurements from the foot, shank, and thigh IMUs, the insole, and the hip and knee encoders, and let the target $\mathbf{y}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$ hold mass-normalized biological hip and knee moments.
During deployment, an unknown corruption $f\in\mathcal{F}$ may transform the observations and produce $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1:T}$.
We seek a predictor $\widehat{\mathbf{y}}_t$ and a reliability gate $g_t\in[0,1]$ such that the commanded torque
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\tau}^{\mathrm{cmd}}_t
    = g_t\,\mathcal{C}(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}_{1:t})
\end{equation}
preserves useful assistance for clean inputs while suppressing harmful commands under task shifts or sensor faults, where $\mathcal{C}$ is the baseline pipeline for moment-to-torque scaling, delay, filtering, and saturation.
Evaluation must cover moment fidelity, fault discrimination, command attenuation, retained baseline assistance, and smoothness; none of these criteria suffices alone.

\subsection{Reliability-Aware Causal TCN}

Training-participant channel means $\mathbf{c}$ and standard deviations $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ normalize each observation as $\mathbf{z}_t=(\mathbf{x}_t-\mathbf{c})\oslash\max(\mathbf{v},10^{-6})$, with $\oslash$ elementwise division.
Following \citet{molinaro2024task} and \citet{bai2018tcn}, the shared encoder has $L=5$ residual blocks, each with two causal convolutions of kernel size $k=5$ and dilation $2^{\ell-1}$, weight normalization, ReLU, and dropout.
The receptive field is $H=1+2(k-1)\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}2^{\ell-1}=249$ samples, and the first $H-1$ window outputs are excluded from training and evaluation.

Five $1\!\times\!1$ heads produce the moment mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$, log variance $\mathbf{s}_t$ (clamped to $[-8,5]$), binary fault logit $a_t$, clean-input reconstruction $\widehat{\mathbf{z}}_t$, and $h$-step forecast $\overline{\mathbf{z}}_{t+h\mid t}$, giving the diagonal predictive distribution
\begin{equation}
    p(\mathbf{y}_t\mid\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1:t})
    = \mathcal{N}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t,
      \operatorname{diag}(\exp\mathbf{s}_t)\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Fault-Augmented Multi-Task Learning}

For each training window, we sample clean data or one of six faults through an operator $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\mathcal{A}_f(\mathbf{x};\boldsymbol{\xi})$ at fixed severity and target group: the insole or encoder group is zeroed; the shank IMU takes a bias ramp of $0.25$ channel standard deviations; packet loss holds each sample independently with probability $0.15$ across all groups; the foot IMU freezes; or sensor delay shifts every group by $10$ samples ($50$~ms), scaled by $1.0$, $1.6$, $0.6$, $2.0$, and $1.2$ for foot, shank, thigh, insole, and encoder, so modalities become mutually asynchronous rather than uniformly late.
Each fault occupies its whole window, so the fault label $b_f$ below is window-constant.
The three held-out operators reuse known fault families at the same nominal rates: burst loss drops runs of $\max(2,\operatorname{round}(2+24p))$ samples at rate $p$, partial loss draws a per-channel mask, and delay jitter redraws the shift per window in $[0.5,1.5]$ times nominal.
They test operator transfer within known packet-loss and delay semantics, not open-set hardware-fault generalization; the supplement lists every protocol role.

With $m_{jt}$ indicating a valid moment label and $b_f=\mathbb{I}[f\neq\mathrm{clean}]$, the masked heteroscedastic loss is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mom}}
&=\frac{1}{2\sum_{j,t}m_{jt}}\sum_{j,t}m_{jt}\,\ell_{jt},\\
\ell_{jt}&=s_{jt}+(y_{jt}-\mu_{jt})^2\exp(-s_{jt}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The moment head receives $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}$ with the clean target $\mathbf{y}$, encouraging robustness when a fault leaves enough information.
The fault head uses timestepwise binary cross-entropy:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fault}}=-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{t\in\Omega}
\big[&b_f\log\sigma(a_t)\\
&+(1-b_f)\log(1-\sigma(a_t))\big],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega$ contains the timesteps with at least one valid joint label.

The auxiliary heads recover the uncorrupted normalized sequence instead of reproducing the corrupted input:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}} & = \operatorname{MSE}_{\Omega}
\left(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}_t(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}),\mathbf{z}_t(\mathbf{x})\right),\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}} & = \operatorname{MSE}_{\Omega}
\left(\overline{\mathbf{z}}_{t+h\mid t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1:t}),\mathbf{z}_{t+h}(\mathbf{x})\right).
\end{align}
The complete objective is $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mom}}+\lambda_f\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fault}}+\lambda_r\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}+\lambda_p\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}$ with $(\lambda_f,\lambda_r,\lambda_p)=(0.2,0.1,0.1)$.
Reconstruction updates the shared encoder, whereas forecasting stops gradients at $\mathbf{h}_t$ so that it acts as a temporal probe.

\subsection{Complementary Integrity Signals}

Different faults affect different signal properties, so the monitor considers eight candidates. The learned ones are fault probability $q_t^{\mathrm{fault}}=\sigma(a_t)$, mean aleatoric scale $q_t^{\mathrm{ale}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_j\exp(s_{jt}/2)$, reconstruction and forecast residuals, and optional epistemic scale.
For a modality group $G$, the residual scores are
\begin{align}
q_t^{\mathrm{rec}} &= \max_G\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{c\in G}
(\widehat z_{ct}-\widetilde z_{ct})^2,\\
q_{t+h}^{\mathrm{pred}} &= \max_G\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{c\in G}
(\overline z_{c,t+h\mid t}-\widetilde z_{c,t+h})^2.
\end{align}
A forecast made at $t$ is scored only when its target arrives at $t+h$, so the score at $u$ compares $\overline{\mathbf{z}}_{u\mid u-h}$ with $\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}_u$ and the channel is zero for the first $h$ samples, preserving command-time causality. Epistemic scale is the joint-averaged standard deviation across stochastic-dropout passes or ensemble members.

Three causal statistics cover signatures that learned heads may miss. Staleness is the moving fraction of nearly unchanged channels, and coherence is one minus the mean absolute rolling correlation between differenced, corresponding IMU axes; like staleness it uses a trailing window, so its value at $t$ needs no future sample. Drift is the largest modality-averaged channel slope, fitted over the whole window and therefore the only genuinely window-level channel.
The gate we evaluate consumes staleness but not coherence or drift, so both appear as diagnostic channels: for drift this is forced by its definition, whereas for coherence it is an implementation choice of the evaluated system, and the severity analysis below quantifies its cost. Of the two, only coherence is ever selected.

\subsection{Validation-Normalized Fault Evidence}

For validation window $i$, each channel is averaged over its valid timesteps $\Omega_i$ to obtain $Q_{ik}=|\Omega_i|^{-1}\sum_{t\in\Omega_i}q_{it}^{k}$. The detector reference is $\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}}=\operatorname{Quantile}_{0.9}(\{Q_{ik}:i\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{clean}}\})$ over clean validation windows, and a subset $S$ fuses as $R_i^{\mathrm{det}}(S)=\max_{k\in S}Q_{ik}/\max(\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}},10^{-6})$.
Using five validation faults (insole missing, encoder dropout, IMU bias, packet loss, and sensor delay), we enumerate subsets of at most three channels and, given their validation-fault AUROCs $A_f(S)$, maximize
\begin{equation}
    J_{\mathrm{det}}(S)=\operatorname{mean}_f A_f(S)
    \!+\!0.25\min_f A_f(S)\!-\!0.002(|S|\!-\!1),
\end{equation}
where the second term rewards the hardest fault and the last favors smaller subsets.
Both $S$ and $\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}}$ are frozen before test evaluation, and burst loss, partial-channel loss, and delay jitter are excluded from training and detector selection alike.

Coherence and drift sit outside the evaluated gate, so one fused number would overstate what is available to deployment.
We run the same subset search twice on the same validation scores: \emph{Detector-all} searches all eight candidates as an offline diagnostic reference, while \emph{Detector-gate} restricts the pool to the channels actually implemented in $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}$ below.
These subsets are used only for window-level AUROC evaluation.
The gate does not reuse the selected detector subset: it fuses all available members of $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}$ and tunes the actuation mapping separately.

\subsection{Validation-Selected Assistance Gate}

The command-time anomaly score uses the causal timestep-level channels
$\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}=\{\mathrm{fault},\mathrm{ale},\mathrm{rec},\mathrm{pred},\mathrm{epi},\mathrm{stale}\}$ that are available in a given configuration:
\begin{equation}
    r_t=\max_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate}}}
    q_t^k/\max(\rho_k^{\mathrm{gate}},10^{-6}).
\end{equation}
We share the detector scale, $\rho_k^{\mathrm{gate}}:=\rho_k^{\mathrm{det}}$, to place window- and timestep-level scores on the same numerical scale; it is a quantile of window means, not a timestep quantile or a calibrated posterior probability. Clean training-held-out-task validation trials and designated validation faults select the deadband $d$ and softness $\gamma$.

The score is converted into a continuous gain:
\begin{equation}
g_t = \operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(
1-\frac{r_t-(1+d)}{\gamma}\right),
\end{equation}
so $g_t=1$ within the nominal region $r_t\leq1+d$ and decreases linearly beyond it.
Starting from $d_0=0.1$, we set
\begin{equation}
    d=\min\!\left(d_{\max},\max\left[d_0,
    \operatorname{Quantile}_{0.9}(r^{\mathrm{val\text{-}OOD}})-1\right]\right),
\end{equation}
with $d_{\max}=2$, using clean training-held-out-task trials from validation participants.
We then search $\gamma\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2,4,8\}$.
The archived evaluation required at least 95\% clean aligned-command retention and recorded non-increase checks on the clean wrong-direction fraction and four validation faults (insole missing, encoder dropout, packet loss, and sensor delay).
Writing $\epsilon=10^{-6}$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{clean}}$ for gated-over-ungated clean retention, and, over validation faults, $\bar\delta$ for the mean relative wrong-direction reduction
$\min(1,\max(0,(w^{\mathrm{ungated}}-w^{\mathrm{gated}})/\max(w^{\mathrm{ungated}},\epsilon)))$ and $\bar\kappa$ for the same retention ratio, candidates are ranked by
\begin{equation}
    J_{\mathrm{gate}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{clean}} + 2\bar\delta + 0.25\,\bar\kappa,
\end{equation}
This exactly describes the reported operating point, but the non-increase checks are not substantive constraints: because $g_t\geq0$, the gate cannot create a sign reversal.
Thus, the only active feasibility condition is clean retention and $\bar\delta$ ranks hard closures; we do not interpret this selection rule as a safety-constrained optimization.
The baseline transformation $\mathcal{C}$ scales the estimated hip and knee moments by $0.20$ and $0.15$, applies the configured joint delays and a 6-Hz low-pass filter, and clips torque to $0.45$ Nm/kg; the gated command $\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\mathrm{cmd}}_t=g_t\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1:t})$ attenuates unreliable estimates but does not replace independent actuator saturation or hardware limits.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Data, Splits, and Comparisons}

We use the released exoskeleton sensor and biological-moment data underlying the task-agnostic controller \citep{molinaro2024task,scherpereel2023dataset}, resampled to a uniform $200$~Hz, so one sample is $5$~ms and the forecast horizon $h=1$ is one control step.
Inputs comprise $25$ unilateral channels in five modality groups $G$: foot, shank, and thigh IMU ($6$ channels each, three gyroscope and three accelerometer axes), insole ($3$), and hip--knee encoders ($4$), with the action profiles of the input ablation adding a sixth group; targets are right hip and knee moments in Nm/kg.
Of the $15$ participants, $11$ train, $2$ validate, and $2$ test. Jumping, cutting, weight lifting, and lunges are excluded from estimator fitting and form the training-held-out task set; clean trials from these categories are used only to tune the gate on validation participants. Thus, they are not unseen to the complete pipeline. No test participant informs preprocessing, score normalization, detector selection, or gate tuning. The leave-one-subject-out analysis rotates over all $15$, each fold taking the preceding participant as validation, and the primary baseline is a deterministic, non-augmented TCN on the same split.
Ablations add fault augmentation, probabilistic regression, reconstruction, and forecasting in order, with additional dropout, ensemble, action-input, severity, and leave-one-subject-out analyses.

\subsection{Metrics and Implementation}

We report RMSE, MAE, $R^2$, Gaussian NLL, uncertainty--error correlation, interval coverage and calibration error, and per-channel and fused fault AUROC. Control-replay metrics are defined against the ideal command $\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\star}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{y})$, the \emph{true} moment through the same transformation $\mathcal{C}$.
A joint-timestep pair $n$ is \emph{active} when its label is valid and $|\tau^{\star}_n|>0.02$ Nm/kg; the set $\mathcal{A}$ depends only on the true moment, so it is identical for gated and ungated commands and cannot be shrunk by attenuation.
Writing $u_n=\tau^{\mathrm{cmd}}_n$, our primary command-consequence metric is the opposition-weighted torque-product integral
\begin{equation}
X_{\mathrm{opp}}(u)=\textstyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}}
\max(0,-u_n\tau^{\star}_n)\,\Delta t,\quad \Delta t=1/200\ {\rm s}.
\end{equation}
Its units are $(\mathrm{Nm/kg})^2\mathrm{s}$; because no angular velocity appears, it is an exposure surrogate, not mechanical energy.
We additionally report the wrong-direction fraction
$w(u)=|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{I}[u_n\tau_n^\star<0]$, peak $|u_n|$ over those pairs, and mean absolute torque rate $|\Delta u|/\Delta t$ in Nm\,kg$^{-1}$\,s$^{-1}$.
Command retention is
\begin{equation}
\kappa=\frac{\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}}\max(0,u_n^{\mathrm{gated}}\operatorname{sgn}\tau_n^\star)}
{\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}}\max(0,u_n^{\mathrm{ungated}}\operatorname{sgn}\tau_n^\star)} .
\end{equation}
It is a weighted fraction of the baseline's same-direction command, not a measure of tracking accuracy; same-direction over-assistance is not rewarded by the ratio because the denominator uses the identical ungated command.
Since $u_n^{\mathrm{gated}}=g_nu_n^{\mathrm{ungated}}$ and $g_n\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
w(u^{\mathrm{gated}})
={}&|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}\!\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}}
\mathbb{I}[u_n^{\mathrm{ungated}}\tau_n^\star<0]\\[-2pt]
&{}\times\mathbb{I}[g_n>0]
\leq w(u^{\mathrm{ungated}}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Thus, $w$ responds only to exact shutdowns ($g_n=0$), whereas $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ responds to every attenuation $0\leq g_n<1$.
A first-order actuator proxy also estimates net-moment RMS and work-related quantities, as relative offline comparisons rather than metabolic outcomes.
The model has five 80-channel TCN blocks, kernel size 5, dropout 0.15, and 768-sample windows at stride 384 with the first 248 outputs masked, trained for 20 epochs with AdamW ($10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) under seeds 7, 13, and 23; Gaussian NLL omits the additive $\frac12\log(2\pi)$ constant. We report mean, standard deviation, and paired seed-level differences.
Three seeds quantify only training stochasticity, not participant uncertainty. The interval for the cross-subject wrong-direction analysis instead comes from the fifteen leave-one-subject-out folds, as a percentile bootstrap $95\%$ interval over folds ($10{,}000$ resamples, fixed seed) on each subject's reduction macro-averaged over nine faults and both task splits.
Over ten scenarios, two splits, and several metrics, an uncorrected test family would be uninterpretable and a corrected one underpowered at three seeds, so we report no per-scenario tests: those entries are descriptive, and the main inferential statement rests on the between-subject interval.

\subsection{Fault Detection under Task Shift}

Figure~\ref{fig:detection-taxonomy} reports all nine operators and all eight candidate channels. Across the six training-time faults, Detector-gate reaches macro-AUROC $0.815\pm0.006$ on ID and $0.740\pm0.002$ on training-held-out tasks, against $0.849\pm0.005$ and $0.773\pm0.002$ for Detector-all; for the three held-out operators the pairs are $0.812\pm0.011$ and $0.715\pm0.002$ against $0.842\pm0.010$ and $0.778\pm0.004$.
The gap is concentrated rather than uniform: validation selects $\{$reconstruction residual, staleness, coherence$\}$ for Detector-all and $\{$reconstruction residual, staleness$\}$ for Detector-gate, and losing coherence costs fixed sensor delay $0.878\to0.628$ on ID and $0.774\to0.557$ on training-held-out tasks, with delay jitter behaving similarly ($0.867\to0.641$, $0.766\to0.559$).
Coherence also reverses direction for held-out burst loss (AUROC $0.055$ ID and $0.054$ on training-held-out tasks), exposing the high-score-is-anomalous assumption of max fusion rather than supporting an open-set claim.
Elsewhere the smaller subset is no worse, and burst packet loss improves from $0.743$ to $0.886$ on ID tasks because coherence is inverted there rather than uninformative: at $0.055$ AUROC its clean-side scores sit above its faulty ones, and a maximum-fusion inherits that raised clean baseline.
Averaged over all nine faults the strongest single channel is staleness ($0.785$ on ID and $0.796$ on training-held-out tasks); coherence is the most extreme rather than the broadest, reaching $1.000$ for partial channel loss and a stuck IMU but only $0.658$ and $0.663$ on average, while IMU bias stays the hardest fused case at $0.502$ and $0.504$, close to chance and so nearly indistinguishable from valid motion with these sensors.

\subsection{Control Consequences, Retention, and Smoothness}

Figure~\ref{fig:safety-utility} makes $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$, rather than the hard-shutdown-sensitive count $w$, the primary consequence view.
On training-held-out tasks under training-time faults, $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ falls from $0.0439\pm0.0062$ to $0.0298\pm0.0030$ $(\mathrm{Nm/kg})^2\mathrm{s}$; for held-out operators it falls from $0.0412\pm0.0066$ to $0.0270\pm0.0041$. Peak wrong-direction torque also drops from $0.175\pm0.025$ to $0.137\pm0.017$ Nm/kg on training-held-out seen faults.
The effect is smaller on ID faults ($0.0581\to0.0469$ for seen faults), negligible on clean ID trials ($0.0408\to0.0400$), and substantial on clean training-held-out trials ($0.0316\to0.0236$), showing that the gate also attenuates ordinary task-shift error.

To separate these effects, we subtract the clean gate change within each seed and task split before macro-averaging the nine matched fault scenarios.
The fault-scenario change in $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ is $-0.0114\pm0.0020$; the matched clean change is $-0.0044\pm0.0009$, leaving a clean-adjusted contrast of $-0.0070\pm0.0015$ $(\mathrm{Nm/kg})^2\mathrm{s}$.
For $w$, the corresponding values are $-0.736\pm0.067$, $-0.397\pm0.043$, and $-0.339\pm0.098$ percentage points, so only about $46\%$ of the count reduction lies beyond the clean-condition effect.
This is a seed- and split-matched aggregate contrast, not a window-level paired interval.

The attenuation has two visible costs. Clean aligned-command retention is $0.960$ ID and $0.961$ on training-held-out tasks, but falls to $0.829$--$0.948$ under faults.
Moreover, mean absolute torque rate rises from $0.237$ to $0.279$ Nm\,kg$^{-1}$\,s$^{-1}$ on training-held-out seen faults and $0.249$ to $0.318$ for held-out operators, approximately $18\%$ and $28\%$.
Because $\dot u=g\dot u_0+u_0\dot g$, the second term can add discontinuities; without a rate limiter or hysteresis, the current gate cannot support an online safety claim.

\subsection{Ablations and Stress Tests}

The ordered, non-factorial ablation adds fault augmentation, probabilistic regression, reconstruction, and forecasting under a fixed split and budget. Successive Detector-all AUROC changes are $+0.042\pm0.036$, $-0.083\pm0.003$, $+0.024\pm0.004$, and $-0.001\pm0.008$. Thus the simpler stage-2 detector ranks highest by AUROC; reconstruction is only the first stage whose selected score activates the gate. These results diagnose the fixed sequence rather than identify a Pareto-optimal architecture.

At maximum packet loss, Detector-all and Detector-gate reach $0.961$ ID and $0.914$ training-held-out AUROC; at the longest delay they diverge to $0.899/0.743$ and $0.788/0.588$, respectively, because only the diagnostic coherence channel responds strongly. Supplementary device-input and ensemble analyses show no consistent fusion gain. Across 15 leave-one-subject-out folds, the per-subject wrong-direction reduction averages $0.44$ percentage points (bootstrap $95\%$ interval $[0.23,0.67]$); all folds improve, with mean clean retention $0.961$ and minimum $0.880$. The supplement reports full stress, ablation, and fold-level results.

\section{Discussion}
Reliability estimates matter only if they change the command rather than serving as confidence scores.
The validation-selected gate yields a clean-adjusted $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$ reduction of $0.0070$ $(\mathrm{Nm/kg})^2\mathrm{s}$ while retaining $96.1\%$ of the clean baseline's aligned command: a modest offline effect at one operating point.
The wrong-direction count captures only exact shutdowns, and the simultaneous torque-rate increase exposes a trade-off that AUROC or retention alone would hide.

Fault mechanisms violate different signal properties: reconstruction is most informative for a missing insole on ID tasks, staleness for packet loss and for the insole under task shift, and coherence for partial channel loss and delay, so fusion covers more cases than any single channel.
Some auxiliary heads nonetheless reduce the aggregate well outside seed noise, and clean RMSE is worse than the split-matched deterministic TCN, so predictive uncertainty and auxiliary training are not universal solutions.
The layer complements approaches that expand behavioral coverage \citep{molinaro2024task} or reduce training-data demands \citep{scherpereel2025domain}, but score references and the operating point must be re-estimated in each target sensor domain.


\section{Limitations}
The score responds to prediction error in general. Our clean-adjusted contrast separates the average task-shift component at the seed/split level, but archived aggregates cannot provide window-level paired uncertainty.
Because nonnegative gating makes $w(u^{\mathrm{gated}})\leq w(u^{\mathrm{ungated}})$ identically, the reported selection rule's wrong-direction non-increase checks are vacuous; a revised operating point must be reselected using $X_{\mathrm{opp}}$, false-shutdown or tracking constraints, and torque rate.
The current comparison also lacks retention- and full-closure-matched random gates, component-only gates, an oracle error gate, and a smoothed or hysteretic gate, so it does not isolate the value of multi-signal fusion or continuous attenuation.
The ordered ablation is not factorial and does not establish that the full model is Pareto-optimal; in fact, the simpler stage-2 detector has higher AUROC.

All corruptions occupy the full 768-sample window. Consequently, window AUROC does not test onset latency, recovery, transient faults, alarm chatter, simultaneous faults, or confusion between valid pauses and frozen sensors.
The three held-out operators remain within known packet-loss and delay families, and synthetic replay cannot represent the hardware failure distribution.
We did not run the gate online on a physical exoskeleton; fixed-kinematics replay supports relative command comparisons, not interaction safety, human adaptation, or metabolic benefit.
The measured torque-rate increase requires rate limiting or hysteresis and hardware validation before deployment.
Labels and sensing come from one exoskeleton platform, and we do not jointly train the domain-adaptation model of \citet{scherpereel2025domain}.
The LOSO bootstrap treats held-out participants as resampling units despite overlapping training sets, so its interval is conditional on this cohort and pipeline rather than a population-level safety guarantee.
Finally, the actual gate fuses all six available channels while the reported Detector-gate AUROC uses a selected subset; detector AUROC is therefore indirect evidence for the actuation score. Independent low-level torque and hardware limits remain necessary.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a causal reliability evaluation that connects heterogeneous anomaly evidence to offline command consequences. The gate produces a modest clean-adjusted reduction in opposition exposure, but worsened nominal estimation, hard-shutdown-sensitive counts, missing matched baselines, and increased torque rate preclude a safety claim. The next required step is to reselect and test a rate-limited gate with matched controls, transient and real faults, and online human-subject validation.

\section{Ethical Statement}

Reliability-aware assistance may reduce risk from silent sensing failures, but overconfident claims could encourage premature deployment around human users, so we distinguish offline replay from human-subject evidence, report negative and weak detector results, and do not present the gate as a safety guarantee. Released participant data must be handled under the source dataset's consent conditions.
